\begin{abstract}
This article presents a dwell constrained robust invariant set (RIS) framework for the safe weathervaning control of an autonomous surface vessel (ASV) propelled by a diesel engine through a sterndrive. Since ASVs are commonly underactuated systems, such as those driven by a single sterndrive, weathervaning control is adopted to hold the ASV inside a region around the setpoint while its heading turns freely against the environmental load. However, the diesel engine cannot reverse its rotation, so the thrust is reversed by a gearbox through neutral. Once a gear is engaged, the propeller delivers at least a minimum thrust, forming a deadzone around zero thrust, so fine corrections require shifting. Each shift locks the gear for a minimum dwell, and a lock in neutral gives the thrust vectoring sterndrive neither thrust nor steering while wind and current keep acting. Existing methods have not guaranteed that a nonlinear ASV stays inside the region under such a dwell and disturbances. To close this gap, Hamilton Jacobi (HJ) reachability is applied to a state augmented with the gear and its remaining lock time. The dwell constrained safety value is learned by adversarial reinforcement learning with legality masking on a semi Markov decision process that holds each gear decision over the dwell, and the learned safety policy controls the ASV. The proposed framework is validated through simulations and full scale field experiments on an 8 m ASV, where the proposed controller kept the ASV closer to the setpoint with shorter excursions than the baseline controllers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec_intro}

\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} surface vessels (ASVs) have been widely applied to environmental monitoring, hydrographic survey, maritime rescue, coastal surveillance, and maritime transportation \cite{liu2016,shi2017,lee2024canal}. Many of these missions, including water column profiling, greenhouse gas sampling, and water quality mapping \cite{smith2010cinaps,dunbabin2017ghg,kaizu2011airboat}, require the ASV to remain near a target point for some time \cite{chavezgalaviz2024station}. Vessels in inland waterway transport also often have to wait until the passage ahead is cleared, for example at a movable bridge \cite{segovia2025bridges}. During these phases, wind and current continuously push the ASV away from the target point, and its safety depends on keeping the position inside the region around the point.

Keeping the position against these disturbances is demanding for many ASVs because of their propulsion layout. Common layouts are two fixed stern thrusters with differential thrust or a single sterndrive, and the ASV of this study has a single engine, a single sterndrive, and no lateral thruster. Such ASVs are underactuated and cannot counter the sway drift induced by wind, waves, and current \cite{dunbabin2017ghg,qu2022positioning}. Therefore, holding the position and heading together is difficult \cite{chavezgalaviz2024station}, and no smooth static state feedback can stabilize both at fixed values \cite{pettersen2000underactuated,pettersen2004guas}. Weathervaning control is a practical alternative, in which only the position is kept inside a region around the setpoint, as if the ASV were anchored \cite{sarda2016stationkeeping}, while the heading turns toward the mean environmental load. This idea has been widely adopted in weather optimal positioning of dynamically positioned ships \cite{fossen2001wopc,kim2017coordinated,kim2014weathervaning} and has been extended to underactuated ASVs by aligning the bow with the wind \cite{smith2010cinaps,pereira2008stationkeeping} or by adapting the heading against the mean load \cite{wang2022areakeeping,qu2022positioning}. In these studies, the thrust is a continuous control input.

Electric propulsion suits such continuous inputs, since an electric motor varies its torque rapidly and reverses its rotation directly \cite{kirtley2015motors,geertsma2017hybrid}. Nevertheless, many ASVs use internal combustion engines because batteries limit the endurance and payload of larger and faster hulls \cite{zhuang2021highspeed,chiche2021rescue}. Examples include a diesel waterjet USV \cite{han2020aragon}, an ASV with a steerable outboard engine \cite{eriksen2019bcmpc}, and a gasoline airboat \cite{kaizu2011airboat}. In such vessels, the engine cannot reverse its rotation, so the propeller thrust is reversed through a reversing gearbox that selects reverse, neutral, or forward \cite{geertsma2017hybrid}. Once a gear is engaged, the engine runs at least at idle, so the propeller delivers a minimum thrust and a deadzone forms around zero thrust. Therefore, a small corrective thrust cannot be produced, and fine corrections near the setpoint must be made by engaging and disengaging the gear, which makes the shift and its dwell frequent. Every shift takes a finite time and repeated shifts stress the gearbox, so a minimum interval between consecutive shifts protects the drivetrain. The sterndrive of this study is such a drive, and its actuation system enforces this interval as a minimum dwell (Section~\ref{sec_dwell}). Since the sterndrive steers by vectoring the thrust, a lock in neutral leaves the ASV with neither thrust nor steering under wind and current. The weathervaning controllers above treat the thrust as a continuous input, so the gear levels, the deadzone, and the dwell have not been considered.

Dwell time constraints have been addressed within model predictive control (MPC) for systems with discrete actuators, a widely studied framework \cite{bemporad1999mld,rawlings2017discrete,mcallister2022advances}. For linear switched systems, dwell time and tube based MPC \cite{zhang2016,tan2024asyncmdt,vicente2019,lavaei2021,zhuang2023} treats the switching signal as an exogenous input and the minimum dwell as an admissibility condition. For linear systems with disturbances, invariant sets under a dwell time restriction have also been computed \cite{dehghan2012}. For nonlinear systems, MPC based on combinatorial integral approximation (CIA) \cite{sager2011,buerger2019switched,zeile2021dwell,buerger2020pycombina} makes the integer input a decision variable and imposes the dwell as a combinatorial constraint in the rounding step. A sum up rounding scheme further establishes recursive feasibility through an $\ell$ step control invariant terminal set \cite{chenlazar2022}. However, these schemes rely on a nominal model, and mixed integer MPC offers only inherent robustness to small disturbances \cite{mcallister2022advances}. As a result, safety under a dwell and disturbances has not been guaranteed for nonlinear systems.

Safety of nonlinear systems under disturbances can be guaranteed by Hamilton Jacobi (HJ) reachability, which poses the problem as a zero sum game between the control and the disturbance \cite{mitchell2005time,bansal2017}. The conventional computation solves the HJ partial differential equation by dynamic programming on a state space grid, whose cost grows exponentially with the state dimension \cite{bansal2017}. This curse of dimensionality makes the computation intractable for high dimensional systems. To overcome this limitation, learning based methods approximate the value function with a neural network \cite{bansal2021deepreach} or learn it by reinforcement learning \cite{fisac2019,yu2022,li2023cdc,li2309dual,li2311decomp}. These methods deliver a value function whose zero superlevel set is a robust invariant set (RIS). However, all of them assume that the full control set is available at every step. When the dwell is ignored, the value is computed with physically infeasible actions, such as reversing the thrust during a lock. Therefore, the certificate is optimistic and declares states safe that cannot be held under the dwell.

In this study, we develop a dwell constrained RIS framework for weathervaning control of an ASV driven by a single sterndrive. Under wind and current, the framework keeps the ASV inside a specified region around the setpoint with its heading free, while the gear thrust is restricted by the deadzone and the dwell. To account for these restrictions, the safety problem is formulated by HJ reachability on the state augmented with the gear and its remaining lock time, which determine the legal gear commands. The dwell constrained safety value is learned by adversarial reinforcement learning with legality masking on a semi Markov decision process (SMDP), in which each gear decision is held over the dwell. This masking excludes illegal shifts in both the simulated plant and the value update. The learned safety policy then selects the legal gear and the steering command that maximize the certified value, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig_framework}. This policy runs onboard the real full scale ASV and is validated in sea trials under the physical gear shift dwell.

To the best of our knowledge, this study represents the first field demonstration of a learned robust safety certificate on a full scale ASV. The main contributions of this article are summarized as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A dwell constrained RIS is formulated by HJ reachability for weathervaning control, in which the gear and its remaining lock time are augmented to the state.
\item An adversarial learning scheme on an SMDP with legality masking is developed for the dwell constrained safety value, and the resulting safety policy controls the ASV.
\item The proposed framework is validated through simulations and full scale field experiments on an 8 m sterndrive ASV.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec_problem} formulates the augmented safety game. Section~\ref{sec_method} presents the proposed dwell constrained safe controller. Section~\ref{sec_results} reports the system identification, simulation, and experimental results. Finally, Section~\ref{sec_conclusion} concludes this article.

\section{System Description and Modeling}\label{sec_system}

This section describes the gear shift dwell and the thrust deadzone of the sterndrive, together with the vehicle dynamics model and the disturbance set.
\subsection{Gear Shift Dwell and Thrust Deadzone}\label{sec_dwell}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_gear.pdf}
\caption{Gear shift restrictions of the sterndrive. Left, deadzone around zero thrust caused by the idle minimum thrust. Right, gear over time with the lock of $D_g$ steps after each shift.}
\label{fig_gear}
\end{figure}

A shift between forward and reverse passes through neutral, and the clutch of the sterndrive gearbox must engage at low engine speed to avoid shock loading of the gear set. Therefore, each shift requires a finite mechanical time, and an immediate reversal of a shift that has just been completed stresses the drivetrain. To protect the drivetrain, the actuation system enforces a minimum interval between consecutive shift commands, which is set by adding a safety margin to the mechanical shift time. This interval is the dwell, and it is given by the actuation system and cannot be shortened by the controller. With the control period $\Delta t$, the dwell corresponds to $D_g$ control steps. A shift request issued during the dwell is discarded by the actuation system, and the current gear is held. Therefore, the controller must account for the time at which a shift becomes available, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig_gear}.

Once a gear is engaged, the engine runs at least at idle and the propeller delivers a minimum thrust. Therefore, thrust magnitudes between zero in neutral and this minimum cannot be produced, which forms a deadzone around zero thrust. Together with the dwell, the deadzone makes the thrust discrete, as modeled in Section~\ref{sec_model}.

\subsection{Vehicle Dynamics Modeling}\label{sec_model}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\IfFileExists{figures/fig_frames.pdf}{\includegraphics[width=0.6\linewidth]{figures/fig_frames.pdf}}{\figph{Coordinate frames and sterndrive geometry.}{4cm}}
\caption{Coordinate frames and sterndrive geometry. Inertial and body fixed frames, the keep in disk of radius $R$ around the setpoint, and the thrust $F_g$ vectored by the steering angle $\delta$ at the distance $l_d$ behind the origin.}
\label{fig_frames}
\end{figure}

The three degree of freedom horizontal plane model of \cite{fossen2011} describes the ASV in the body fixed and inertial frames of Fig.~\ref{fig_frames}. Following \cite{lee2024canal}, the model is formulated as
\begin{subequations}\label{eq_model}
\begin{align}
M\dot{\nu} + C(\nu)\nu + D(\nu)\nu &= f_c + w, \\
\dot{\eta} &= J(\psi)\nu, \\
J(\psi) &= \begin{bmatrix} \cos\psi & -\sin\psi & 0 \\ \sin\psi & \cos\psi & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix},
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\nu = [u, v, r]^\top$ and $\eta = [x, y, \psi]^\top$ are the velocity and position vectors, respectively, and $M$, $C(\nu)$, and $D(\nu)$ denote the inertia matrix, the Coriolis and centripetal matrix, and the damping matrix. Here, $f_c = [f_X, f_Y, f_N]^\top$ represents the control forces and moment in each direction, $w = [w_X, w_Y, w_N]^\top$ denotes the environmental disturbance forces and moment bounded in \eqref{eq_wbox}, and $J(\psi)$ is the rotation matrix from the body fixed frame to the inertial frame. In this article, $f_c$ denotes the control vector because $\tau$ is reserved for the audit margin and $\tau_g$ for the remaining lock time.

Assuming that the ASV is symmetric in the $x$ and $y$ directions, $M$, $C(\nu)$, and $D(\nu)$ can be expressed as
\begin{equation}
M = \operatorname{diag}(m_{11}, m_{22}, m_{33}),
\label{eq_mass}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
C(\nu) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & -m_{22} v \\ 0 & 0 & m_{11} u \\ m_{22} v & -m_{11} u & 0 \end{bmatrix},
\label{eq_coriolis}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
D(\nu) &= -\begin{bmatrix} X_u & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & Y_v & Y_r \\ 0 & N_v & N_r \end{bmatrix} \\
&\quad - \begin{bmatrix} X_{u|u|}|u| & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & Y_{v|v|}|v| & Y_{r|r|}|r| \\ 0 & N_{v|v|}|v| & N_{r|r|}|r| \end{bmatrix},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq_damping}
\end{equation}
where $m_{11}$, $m_{22}$, and $m_{33}$ are the mass and moment of inertia including the added mass and added moment of inertia. The coefficients $X_u$, $Y_v$, $Y_r$, $N_v$, and $N_r$ are the linear drag coefficients, and $X_{u|u|}$, $Y_{v|v|}$, $Y_{r|r|}$, $N_{v|v|}$, and $N_{r|r|}$ are the nonlinear drag coefficients, respectively.

By vectoring the propeller thrust through the steering angle of the drive, the control force and moment are expressed as
\begin{equation}
f_c = \begin{bmatrix} f_X \\ f_Y \\ f_N \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} F_g\cos\delta \\ F_g\sin\delta \\ -l_d F_g\sin\delta \end{bmatrix},
\label{eq_force}
\end{equation}
where $F_g$ is the propeller thrust at gear level $g \in \{\gR, \gN, \gF\}$ at the throttle setting used in this study, with $F_{\gN} = 0$ and $F_{\gR} < 0 < F_{\gF}$. Owing to the deadzone of Section~\ref{sec_dwell}, the thrust takes only these three values. Here, $\delta$ denotes the steering angle of the drive, and $l_d$ is the distance from the origin of the body fixed frame to the drive.

The steering angle and the steering rate are constrained by
\begin{equation}
|\delta| \le \delta_{\max}, \qquad |\dot{\delta}| \le \dot{\delta}_{\max},
\label{eq_steer}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_{\max}$ and $\dot{\delta}_{\max}$ denote the maximum steering angle and the maximum steering rate, respectively.

In addition, it is assumed that the unknown and bounded environmental disturbance $w = [w_X, w_Y, w_N]^\top$, which acts on \eqref{eq_model} as body frame forces and a moment, satisfies
\begin{equation}
\calW = \bigl\{ w \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid -\bar{w} \le w \le \bar{w} \bigr\},
\label{eq_wbox}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{w} = [\bar{w}_X, \bar{w}_Y, \bar{w}_N]^\top$ and the inequality holds entry wise.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec_problem}

This section augments the gear state and its remaining lock time to the state, defines the legal action set, and formulates the safety game with its Bellman equation.

\subsection{Augmented State and Legal Action Set}

Under the dwell, the admissible gear command depends on the time elapsed since the last shift. Consider two instants at which the ASV has the same physical state. If the last shift occurred long ago, any gear can be chosen, whereas if a shift has just occurred, the gear is locked until the dwell expires. Since the physical state alone cannot tell which of the two cases applies, the process is not Markov. Therefore, the gear state and its remaining lock time are augmented to the state, which makes the admissible gear commands a function of the state.

Let $(\tilde{x}, \tilde{y}) = (x - x_d, y - y_d)$ be the position error from the setpoint $(x_d, y_d)$, and let $\tilde{\psi}$ be the heading relative to a fixed reference. Let $\tau_g \in \{0, 1, \ldots, D_g - 1\}$ denote the remaining lock time of the shift in control steps, where $\tau_g = 0$ means that the gear may be shifted now. The augmented state is $\xi = (\tilde{x}, \tilde{y}, \tilde{\psi}, u, v, r, \delta, g, \tau_g) \in \Xi$, and the control is $c = (\dot{\delta}_c, g_c)$, where $\dot{\delta}_c \in \calA_\delta = [-\dot{\delta}_{\max}, \dot{\delta}_{\max}]$ and $g_c$ are the steering rate command and the gear command, respectively. The steering rate is used as the control input instead of the steering angle, because the maximizing control of the safety game tends to be bang bang, and a steering angle command would then jump between its limits, which the steering actuator cannot follow. With the rate command, the steering angle in the state is integrated over each control period and saturated at $\pm\delta_{\max}$, so that the steering trajectory remains physically feasible.

The legal action set is given by
\begin{equation}
\calC(\xi) = \calA_\delta \times \calC_g(g, \tau_g), \qquad
\calC_g = \begin{cases} \{g\}, & \tau_g > 0,\\ \{\gR, \gN, \gF\}, & \tau_g = 0, \end{cases}
\label{eq_legal}
\end{equation}
where the steering channel has no dwell constraint, while the gear channel is locked whenever $\tau_g > 0$. Whenever the gear switches to a new level, the timer resets to $D_g - 1$, and otherwise it counts down toward zero. Holding the current gear is always legal, ensuring that $\calC(\xi)$ is nonempty, and it is compact as the product of a finite set and a compact interval. The augmented transition $\xi' = \Phi(\xi, c, w)$ consists of the physical map obtained by integrating \eqref{eq_model} over one control period under the steering limits \eqref{eq_steer} and the legal gear, together with the timer update. A policy $\pi \colon \Xi \to \calA_\delta \times \{\gR, \gN, \gF\}$ is called dwell constrained admissible if $\pi(\xi) \in \calC(\xi)$ for every $\xi \in \Xi$, and the set of such policies is denoted by $\Pi_{\calC}$.


\subsection{Augmented Safety Game}\label{sec_game}

The task is encoded by the keep in function $h(\xi) = R - \sqrt{\tilde{x}^2 + \tilde{y}^2}$, whose zero superlevel set is the disk of radius $R$ around the setpoint. Since $h$ depends only on the position error, the heading is free and the ASV can weathervane. On the augmented state, the safety value function is defined as
\begin{equation}
V_h^{*}(\xi) = \max_{\pi \in \Pi_{\calC}} \min_{\mu} \min_{k \ge 0} h(\xi_k),
\label{eq_value}
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ ranges over nonanticipative disturbance strategies with values in $\calW$ \cite{bansal2017}. The set $\{\xi \mid V_h^{*}(\xi) \ge 0\}$ is the largest set from which the ASV can be kept inside the disk for all time against every admissible disturbance.

By the dynamic programming principle \cite{li2023cdc}, $V_h^{*}$ satisfies the Bellman equation
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V_h^{*}(\xi) = \min\Bigl\{ h(\xi),\ &\max_{g_c \in \calC_g(g, \tau_g)} \max_{\dot{\delta}_c \in \calA_\delta} \\
&\min_{w \in \calW} V_h^{*}\bigl(\Phi(\xi, (\dot{\delta}_c, g_c), w)\bigr) \Bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq_bellman}
\end{equation}

\section{Dwell Constrained Safe Controller}\label{sec_method}

This section presents the learning of the dwell constrained safety value and the onboard safety policy.

\subsection{Adversarial Learning of the Dwell Constrained Safety Value}\label{sec_learning}
The dwell constrained safety value is learned by an adversarial actor critic scheme that follows reachability constrained reinforcement learning \cite{yu2022} and its disturbed extension \cite{li2023cdc}. Neural network critics approximate the safety value and are regressed onto targets that bootstrap the critic values of later states, while a protagonist maximizes and an adversary minimizes the critic.

With a one step backup of \eqref{eq_bellman}, an excursion caused by a lock reaches the current state only through bootstrapped values at the intermediate locked states. The network receives the remaining lock time and blends a locked state with a nearby state that can still escape by shifting. Each bootstrapped value is therefore pulled toward the safe side and becomes the next target, so the error accumulates along the lock. In preliminary training, this backup certified locked states near the disk boundary as safe. Since the gear is not revised until the lock expires, each gear decision is treated as an option \cite{sutton1999options} that holds the selected gear for $D_g$ steps. The steering rate command and the disturbance act at every step, which defines an SMDP whose target unrolls the simulator over the option. The margin $h$ during the lock is then computed from the simulated states, and the learned value enters only at the end. The remaining errors, from this final value and from the learned adversary, are absorbed by the audit margin $\tau$ introduced below.

The undiscounted condition \eqref{eq_bellman} is not a contraction, and a discount $\gamma_h \in (0,1)$ restores it \cite{fisac2019}, as extended to the disturbed game in \cite{li2023cdc}. For a fixed gear $\hat g$, the locked one step operator is defined as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
(\calT_{\hat g} U)(\xi) &= (1-\gamma_h) h(\xi) + \gamma_h \min\Bigl\{ h(\xi), \\
&\qquad \max_{\dot{\delta}_c \in \calA_\delta} \min_{w \in \calW} U\bigl(\Phi(\xi, (\dot{\delta}_c, \hat g), w)\bigr) \Bigr\},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq_locked}
\end{equation}
and the SMDP backup is given by
\begin{equation}
(\calT_h V)(\xi) = \max_{\hat g \in \calC_g(g, \tau_g)} \bigl(\calT_{\hat g}^{D_g} V\bigr)(\xi),
\label{eq_backup}
\end{equation}
where $\calT_{\hat g}^{D_g}$ denotes $D_g$ successive applications of $\calT_{\hat g}$. The backup folds the margin over the locked interval directly and bootstraps only at the next decision among the legal gears. Since holding the selected gear is legal at every step, every policy generated by the backup is dwell constrained admissible. Let $\Pi_{\calC}^{o} \subset \Pi_{\calC}$ denote such option policies, which change the gear command only at decisions separated by at least $D_g$ steps, and let $V_h^{o}$ be \eqref{eq_value} with $\pi$ restricted to $\Pi_{\calC}^{o}$, so that $V_h^{o} \le V_h^{*}$. Throughout, the maximum over the steering rate command in \eqref{eq_locked}, also with $\gamma_h = 1$, is assumed to be attained, as is standard under compact control and disturbance sets and continuous dynamics \cite{bertsekas1972reach,akametalu2024minimum}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem_aug}
Let $h$ be bounded and let $\calT_h$ be defined by \eqref{eq_backup}. Then $\calT_h$ is a monotone $\gamma_h^{D_g}$ contraction in the supremum norm and admits a unique bounded fixed point $V_h$ with $V_h \le h$. Moreover, $V_h^{o}$ satisfies the fixed point equation of \eqref{eq_backup} with $\gamma_h = 1$, and $\calS^{o} = \{\xi \mid V_h^{o}(\xi) \ge 0\}$ is a robust invariant set of the augmented system under dwell constrained admissible policies with $\calS^{o} \subseteq \{\xi \mid V_h^{*}(\xi) \ge 0\}$.
\end{lemma}

The proof is given in the Appendix. The legal set enters \eqref{eq_backup} only through the maximization over a nonempty finite set at each decision, so the dwell does not affect the contraction and shapes only the fixed point. As $\gamma_h$ approaches one, the discounted fixed point is expected to approach $V_h^{o}$, as shown in \cite{li2023cdc} for the one step operator.

The fixed point of \eqref{eq_backup} is learned as summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg_ris}, and every network receives the augmented state with the heading encoded by its sine and cosine and the gear by a one hot vector, so it observes the gear and the timer.

\subsubsection{Safety Critics}
Two safety critics $Q_{h,1}(\xi, c, w)$ and $Q_{h,2}(\xi, c, w)$ with target copies $\bar{Q}_{h,1}$ and $\bar{Q}_{h,2}$ approximate the value of the joint action and disturbance, and the minimum of the two is used wherever a critic value is needed. The regression target is the backward fold of \eqref{eq_backup} along a simulator unroll of $n \ge D_g$ steps from a sampled state $\xi_0$. The committed legal gear is held for $D_g$ steps and the greedy legal gear is chosen afterwards, while the protagonist steering $\pi_h$ and the adversary disturbance $\mu$ act at every step. With the target value $\bar V(\xi) = \max_{g' \in \calC_g(g,\tau_g)} \min_i \bar{Q}_{h,i}(\xi, (\pi_h(\xi), g'), \mu(\xi))$ at the end of the unroll, the target is formulated as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
g_n &= \bar V(\xi_n), \qquad y = g_0, \\
g_j &= (1-\gamma_h) h(\xi_j) + \gamma_h \min\{ h(\xi_j),\ g_{j+1} \},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq_target}
\end{equation}
for $j = n-1, \ldots, 0$, where $\xi_j$ is the state at step $j$ of the unroll. The bootstrap term is clipped to $[-h_{\mathrm{floor}}, R]$ and $h$ is floored at $-h_{\mathrm{floor}}$ outside the disk, ensuring that the value remains bounded as required by Lemma~\ref{lem_aug}. A squared loss regresses the critics onto $y$ at the sampled state and the committed action, and the target copies follow the critics by a soft update.

\subsubsection{Protagonist and Adversary}
The protagonist $\pi_h$ is a deterministic steering rate policy whose output is scaled by $\tanh$ into $\calA_\delta$, and the adversary $\mu$ is a deterministic policy whose output is scaled by $\tanh$ into the box $\calW$. Let $q(\xi, c) = \min_i Q_{h,i}(\xi, c, \mu(\xi))$ denote the minimum critic at the disturbance of the adversary. The greedy legal gear is selected by enumerating the at most three legal gears as
\begin{equation}
g^\star(\xi) = \arg\max_{g' \in \calC_g(g,\tau_g)} q\bigl(\xi, (\pi_h(\xi), g')\bigr).
\label{eq_gear}
\end{equation}
Both policies are trained by deterministic policy gradient. The protagonist maximizes the minimum critic at the greedy legal gear with the objective
\begin{equation}
J_\pi = \mathbb{E}_{\xi}\Bigl[ q\bigl(\xi, (\pi_h(\xi), g^\star(\xi))\bigr) \Bigr],
\label{eq_actor}
\end{equation}
and the adversary minimizes $\min_i Q_{h,i}$ at the action $(\pi_h(\xi), g^\star(\xi))$.

\subsubsection{Curricula and Coverage}
Two curricula are employed to stabilize the adversarial training. The adversary scale is ramped from zero to one, and the discount $\gamma_h$ is ramped toward its final value, allowing the horizon of the certificate to grow as the value estimate improves. Every update resets the $N$ parallel simulators to augmented states sampled over $\Xi$, including the gear level and the timer, which covers states entered in the middle of a lock that the protagonist cannot choose.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Adversarial Learning of the Dwell Constrained Safety Value}
\label{alg_ris}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Initialize critics $Q_{h,1}, Q_{h,2}$, targets $\bar{Q}_{h,1}, \bar{Q}_{h,2}$, protagonist $\pi_h$, adversary $\mu$, adversary scale $\kappa \gets 0$
\FOR{each update}
\STATE Sample a batch of augmented states over $\Xi$, including $g$ and $\tau_g$, in the $N$ parallel simulators
\STATE At each sampled state, choose a random legal committed gear and perturb the steering command and the disturbance $\kappa\,\mu(\xi)$ by exploration noise
\STATE Unroll each simulator for $n$ steps, holding the committed gear for $D_g$ steps and the greedy legal gear \eqref{eq_gear} afterwards
\STATE Form $y$ by the backward fold \eqref{eq_target} with clipping
\STATE Update $Q_{h,1}, Q_{h,2}$ by regression onto $y$
\STATE Update $\pi_h$ by ascending \eqref{eq_actor}
\STATE Update $\mu$ by descending $\min_i Q_{h,i}(\xi, (\pi_h(\xi), g^\star(\xi)), \mu(\xi))$
\STATE Soft update $\bar{Q}_{h,i} \gets (1-\rho)\bar{Q}_{h,i} + \rho Q_{h,i}$
\STATE Advance the curricula on $\kappa$ and $\gamma_h$
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Certified Set}
After training, the certified value is $\hat V_h(\xi) = q(\xi, (\pi_h(\xi), g^\star(\xi)))$ and the certified set is $\hat{\calS} = \{\xi \mid \hat V_h(\xi) \ge 0\}$. The value is represented by neural networks without a bound on the approximation error, and the zero level set of the discounted fixed point is optimistic relative to \eqref{eq_value} for $\gamma_h < 1$. Therefore, every certified rate in this article is reported on the audited level set $\hat{\calS}_\tau = \{\xi \mid \hat V_h(\xi) \ge \tau\}$, whose margin $\tau > 0$ absorbs both effects and is calibrated by the adversarial Monte Carlo audit of Section~\ref{sec_sim}. Since the gear and its remaining lock time are part of the state, the certified set is a family of slices over the physical state that depends on them, and a slice shrinks as the remaining lock time grows because the ASV must survive a longer lock without gear authority.

\subsection{Onboard Safety Controller}\label{sec_onboard_ctrl}

The deployed controller applies $\pi_h(\xi)$ and $g^\star(\xi)$ of \eqref{eq_gear} at each control period without a separate task policy, so a shift is requested only when it is legal and raises the certified value. Since the value is the worst case margin to the boundary of the disk, this policy keeps the ASV away from the boundary while the heading turns freely.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec_conclusion}

This article presented a dwell constrained RIS framework for safe weathervaning control of an internal combustion sterndrive ASV. Since the dwell restricts only the control player, the safe set becomes a family of sets that shrinks with the remaining lock time. With the gear state and remaining lock time augmented to the state, the safety value was learned on an SMDP by adversarial reinforcement learning with legality masking, and the learned safety policy ran onboard. Simulations, \pending{simulation headline to be stated once the simulation numbers are final}, and full scale field experiments validated the proposed framework, which kept the ASV closer to the setpoint than the baseline controllers with short excursions at sea. Future work will quantify the approximation error of the neural representation and extend the field validation beyond a single dwell channel.

For a fixed gear $\hat g$, the locked operator \eqref{eq_locked} is a monotone $\gamma_h$ contraction in the supremum norm by the argument of \cite[Theorem~2]{li2023cdc}, which builds on the discounted construction of \cite{fisac2019}. The instantaneous term $(1-\gamma_h)h$ is fixed, the outer minimum with $h$ is nonexpansive, the minimization over the compact box $\calW$ is nonexpansive, and the maximization over the compact steering interval $\calA_\delta$ is nonexpansive, so for bounded $U$ and $U'$,
\begin{equation}
\|\calT_{\hat g} U - \calT_{\hat g} U'\|_\infty \le \gamma_h \|U - U'\|_\infty ,
\end{equation}
and each operation preserves the pointwise order. Therefore, $\calT_{\hat g}^{D_g}$ is a monotone $\gamma_h^{D_g}$ contraction. The maximum over the nonempty finite set $\calC_g(g, \tau_g)$ preserves monotonicity and is nonexpansive in the supremum norm, so $\calT_h$ in \eqref{eq_backup} is a monotone $\gamma_h^{D_g}$ contraction, and the Banach fixed point theorem on the complete space of bounded functions on $\Xi$ yields the unique bounded fixed point $V_h$. Since each application of $\calT_{\hat g}$ returns at most $h$, the fixed point satisfies $V_h \le h$.

For the undiscounted value, with $\gamma_h = 1$ the locked operator reduces to $(\check\calT_{\hat g} U)(\xi) = \min\{h(\xi), \max_{\dot{\delta}_c \in \calA_\delta} \min_{w \in \calW} U(\Phi(\xi, (\dot{\delta}_c, \hat g), w))\}$. The dynamic programming principle of \eqref{eq_value} over one option, in which the gear is held for $D_g$ steps and the steering command is chosen at every step, gives $V_h^{o} = \max_{\hat g \in \calC_g(g,\tau_g)} \check\calT_{\hat g}^{D_g} V_h^{o}$, which is the fixed point equation of \eqref{eq_backup} with $\gamma_h = 1$. Let $V_h^{o}(\xi) \ge 0$. By compactness, a legal gear $\hat g$ attaining the maximum and a steering command attaining the inner maximum at each step of the option exist. Along this option, every disturbance sequence yields $h(\xi_j) \ge 0$ for $j < D_g$ and $V_h^{o}(\xi_{D_g}) \ge 0$. Repeating the argument from $\xi_{D_g}$ yields an option policy in $\Pi_{\calC}^{o} \subset \Pi_{\calC}$ that keeps the state in $\calS^{o}$ for all time against every disturbance, which establishes the robust invariance of $\calS^{o}$ under dwell constrained admissible policies. Finally, $\calS^{o} \subseteq \{\xi \mid V_h^{*}(\xi) \ge 0\}$ follows from $V_h^{o} \le V_h^{*}$. $\blacksquare$
