\begin{abstract}
This article presents a dwell constrained robust invariant set (DC-RIS) framework for the safe weathervaning control of an autonomous surface vessel (ASV) propelled by a diesel engine through a sterndrive. Since ASVs are commonly underactuated systems, such as those driven by a single sterndrive, weathervaning control is adopted to hold the ASV inside a region around the setpoint while its heading turns freely against the environmental load. However, the diesel engine cannot reverse its rotation, so the thrust is reversed by a gearbox through neutral. Once a gear is engaged, the propeller delivers at least a minimum thrust, forming a deadzone around zero thrust, so fine corrections require shifting. Each shift locks the gear for a minimum dwell, and a lock in neutral gives the thrust vectoring sterndrive neither thrust nor steering while wind and current keep acting. Existing methods have not guaranteed that a nonlinear ASV stays inside the region under such a dwell and disturbances. To close this gap, Hamilton Jacobi (HJ) reachability is applied to a state augmented with the gear and its remaining lock time. The dwell constrained safety value is learned by adversarial reinforcement learning with legality masking and a learning target that unrolls the simulator over the dwell, and the learned safety policy controls the ASV. The proposed framework is validated through simulations and full scale field experiments on an 8 m ASV, where \dcris kept the ASV closer to the setpoint with shorter excursions than the baseline controllers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec_intro}

\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} surface vessels (ASVs) have been widely applied to environmental monitoring, hydrographic survey, maritime rescue, coastal surveillance, and maritime transportation \cite{liu2016,shi2017,lee2024canal}. Many of these missions, including water column profiling, greenhouse gas sampling, and water quality mapping \cite{smith2010cinaps,dunbabin2017ghg,kaizu2011airboat}, require the ASV to remain near a target point for some time \cite{chavezgalaviz2024station}. Vessels in inland waterway transport also often have to wait until the passage ahead is cleared, for example at a movable bridge \cite{segovia2025bridges}. During these phases, wind and current continuously push the ASV away from the target point, and its safety depends on keeping the position inside the region around the point.

Keeping the position against these disturbances is demanding for many ASVs because of their propulsion layout. Common layouts are two fixed stern thrusters with differential thrust or a single sterndrive, and the ASV of this study has a single engine and a single sterndrive and does not use a lateral thruster. Such ASVs are underactuated and cannot counter the sway drift induced by wind, waves, and current \cite{dunbabin2017ghg,qu2022positioning}. Therefore, holding the position and heading together is difficult \cite{chavezgalaviz2024station}, and no smooth static state feedback can stabilize both at fixed values \cite{pettersen2000underactuated,pettersen2004guas}. Weathervaning control is a practical alternative, in which only the position is kept inside a region around the setpoint, as if the ASV were anchored \cite{sarda2016stationkeeping}, while the heading turns toward the mean environmental load. This idea has been widely adopted in weather optimal positioning of dynamically positioned ships \cite{fossen2001wopc,kim2017coordinated,kim2014weathervaning} and has been extended to underactuated ASVs by aligning the bow with the wind \cite{smith2010cinaps,pereira2008stationkeeping} or by adapting the heading against the mean load \cite{wang2022areakeeping,qu2022positioning}. In these studies, the thrust is a continuous control input.

Electric propulsion suits such continuous inputs, since an electric motor varies its torque rapidly and reverses its rotation directly \cite{kirtley2015motors,geertsma2017hybrid}. Nevertheless, many ASVs use internal combustion engines because batteries limit the endurance and payload of larger and faster hulls \cite{zhuang2021highspeed,chiche2021rescue}. Examples include a diesel waterjet USV \cite{han2020aragon}, an ASV with a steerable outboard engine \cite{eriksen2019bcmpc}, and a gasoline airboat \cite{kaizu2011airboat}. In such vessels, the engine cannot reverse its rotation, so the propeller thrust is reversed through a reversing gearbox that selects reverse, neutral, or forward \cite{geertsma2017hybrid}. Once a gear is engaged, the engine runs at least at idle, so the propeller delivers a minimum thrust and a deadzone forms around zero thrust. Therefore, a small corrective thrust cannot be produced, and fine corrections near the setpoint must be made by engaging and disengaging the gear, which makes the shift and its dwell frequent. Every shift takes a finite time and repeated shifts stress the gearbox, so a minimum interval between consecutive shifts protects the drivetrain. The sterndrive of this study is such a drive, and its actuation system enforces this interval as a minimum dwell (Section~\ref{sec_dwell}). Since the sterndrive steers by vectoring the thrust, a lock in neutral leaves the ASV with neither thrust nor steering under wind and current. The weathervaning controllers above treat the thrust as a continuous input, so the gear levels, the deadzone, and the dwell have not been considered.

Dwell time constraints have been addressed within model predictive control (MPC) for systems with discrete actuators, a widely studied framework \cite{bemporad1999mld,rawlings2017discrete,mcallister2022advances}. For linear switched systems, dwell time and tube based MPC \cite{zhang2016,tan2024asyncmdt,vicente2019,lavaei2021,zhuang2023} treats the switching signal as an exogenous input and the minimum dwell as an admissibility condition. For linear systems with disturbances, invariant sets under a dwell time restriction have also been computed \cite{dehghan2012}. For nonlinear systems, MPC based on combinatorial integral approximation (CIA) \cite{sager2011,buerger2019switched,zeile2021dwell,buerger2020pycombina} makes the integer input a decision variable and imposes the dwell as a combinatorial constraint in the rounding step. A sum up rounding scheme further establishes recursive feasibility through an $\ell$ step control invariant terminal set \cite{chenlazar2022}. However, these schemes rely on a nominal model, and mixed integer MPC offers only inherent robustness to small disturbances \cite{mcallister2022advances}. As a result, safety under a dwell and disturbances has not been guaranteed for nonlinear systems.

Safety of nonlinear systems under disturbances can be guaranteed by Hamilton Jacobi (HJ) reachability, which poses the problem as a zero sum game between the control and the disturbance \cite{mitchell2005time,bansal2017}. The conventional computation solves the HJ partial differential equation by dynamic programming on a state space grid, whose cost grows exponentially with the state dimension \cite{bansal2017}. This curse of dimensionality makes the computation intractable for high dimensional systems. To overcome this limitation, learning based methods approximate the value function with a neural network \cite{bansal2021deepreach} or learn it by reinforcement learning \cite{fisac2019,yu2022,li2023cdc,li2309dual,li2311decomp}. These methods deliver a value function whose zero superlevel set is a robust invariant set (RIS). However, all of them assume that the full control set is available at every step. When the dwell is ignored, the value is computed with physically infeasible actions, such as reversing the thrust during a lock. Therefore, the certificate is optimistic and declares states safe that cannot be held under the dwell.

In this study, we develop a dwell constrained RIS (DC-RIS) framework for weathervaning control of an ASV driven by a single sterndrive. Under wind and current, the framework keeps the ASV inside a specified region around the setpoint with its heading free, while the gear thrust is restricted by the deadzone and the dwell. To account for these restrictions, the safety problem is formulated by HJ reachability on the state augmented with the gear and its remaining lock time, which determine the legal gear commands. The dwell constrained safety value is learned by adversarial reinforcement learning with legality masking, in which the learning target unrolls the simulator over the dwell so that the margin during a lock is computed from simulated states. This masking excludes illegal shifts in both the simulated plant and the value update. The learned safety policy then selects the legal gear and the steering command that maximize the certified value, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig_framework}. This policy runs onboard the full scale ASV and is validated in sea trials under the physical gear shift dwell.

To the best of our knowledge, this study represents the first field demonstration of a learned robust safety certificate on a full scale ASV. The main contributions of this article are summarized as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A dwell constrained RIS is formulated by HJ reachability for weathervaning control, in which the gear and its remaining lock time are augmented to the state.
\item An adversarial learning scheme with legality masking and a target unrolled over the dwell is developed for the dwell constrained safety value, and the resulting safety policy controls the ASV.
\item The proposed framework is validated through simulations and full scale field experiments on an 8 m sterndrive ASV.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec_problem} formulates the augmented safety game. Section~\ref{sec_method} presents the proposed dwell constrained safe controller. Section~\ref{sec_results} reports the system identification, simulation, and experimental results. Finally, Section~\ref{sec_conclusion} concludes this article.

\section{System Description and Modeling}\label{sec_system}

This section describes the gear shift dwell and the thrust deadzone of the sterndrive, together with the vehicle dynamics model and the disturbance set.
\subsection{Gear Shift Dwell and Thrust Deadzone}\label{sec_dwell}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_gear.pdf}
\caption{Gear shift restrictions of the sterndrive. Left, deadzone around zero thrust caused by the idle minimum thrust. Right, gear over time with the lock of $D_g$ steps after each shift.}
\label{fig_gear}
\end{figure}

A shift between forward and reverse passes through neutral, and the clutch of the sterndrive gearbox must engage at low engine speed to avoid shock loading of the gear set. Therefore, each shift requires a finite mechanical time, and an immediate reversal of a shift that has just been completed stresses the drivetrain. To protect the drivetrain, the actuation system enforces a minimum interval between consecutive shift commands, which is set by adding a safety margin to the mechanical shift time. This interval is the dwell, and it is given by the actuation system and cannot be shortened by the controller. With the control period $\Delta t$, the dwell corresponds to $D_g$ control steps. A shift request issued during the dwell is discarded by the actuation system, and the current gear is held. Therefore, the controller must account for the time at which a shift becomes available, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig_gear}.

Once a gear is engaged, the engine runs at least at idle and the propeller delivers a minimum thrust. Therefore, thrust magnitudes between zero in neutral and this minimum cannot be produced, which forms a deadzone around zero thrust.

\subsection{Vehicle Dynamics Modeling}\label{sec_model}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\IfFileExists{figures/fig_frames.pdf}{\includegraphics[width=0.6\linewidth]{figures/fig_frames.pdf}}{\figph{Coordinate frames and sterndrive geometry.}{4cm}}
\caption{Coordinate frames and sterndrive geometry. Inertial and body fixed frames, the keep in disk of radius $R$ around the setpoint, and the thrust $F_g$ vectored by the steering angle $\delta$ at the distance $l_d$ behind the origin.}
\label{fig_frames}
\end{figure}

The three degree of freedom horizontal plane model of \cite{fossen2011} describes the ASV in the body fixed and inertial frames of Fig.~\ref{fig_frames}. Following \cite{lee2024canal}, the model is formulated as
\begin{subequations}\label{eq_model}
\begin{align}
M\dot{\nu} + C(\nu)\nu + D(\nu)\nu &= f_c + w, \\
\dot{\eta} &= J(\psi)\nu, \\
J(\psi) &= \begin{bmatrix} \cos\psi & -\sin\psi & 0 \\ \sin\psi & \cos\psi & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix},
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\nu = [u, v, r]^\top$ and $\eta = [x, y, \psi]^\top$ are the velocity and position vectors, respectively, and $M$, $C(\nu)$, and $D(\nu)$ denote the inertia matrix, the Coriolis and centripetal matrix, and the damping matrix. Here, $f_c = [f_X, f_Y, f_N]^\top$ represents the control forces and moment in each direction, $w = [w_X, w_Y, w_N]^\top$ denotes the environmental disturbance forces and moment bounded in \eqref{eq_wbox}, and $J(\psi)$ is the rotation matrix from the body fixed frame to the inertial frame. In this article, $f_c$ denotes the control vector because $\tau$ is reserved for the audit margin and $\tau_g$ for the remaining lock time.

Assuming that the ASV is symmetric in the $x$ and $y$ directions, $M$, $C(\nu)$, and $D(\nu)$ can be expressed as
\begin{equation}
M = \operatorname{diag}(m_{11}, m_{22}, m_{33}),
\label{eq_mass}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
C(\nu) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & -c_{vr} v \\ 0 & 0 & c_{ur} u \\ 0 & c_{uv} u & 0 \end{bmatrix},
\label{eq_coriolis}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
D(\nu) &= -\begin{bmatrix} X_u & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & Y_v & Y_r \\ 0 & N_v & N_r \end{bmatrix} \\
&\quad - \begin{bmatrix} X_{u|u|}|u| & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & Y_{v|v|}|v| & Y_{r|r|}|r| \\ 0 & N_{v|v|}|v| & N_{r|r|}|r| \end{bmatrix},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq_damping}
\end{equation}
where $m_{11}$, $m_{22}$, and $m_{33}$ are the mass and moment of inertia including the added mass and added moment of inertia. The coefficients $X_u$, $Y_v$, $Y_r$, $N_v$, and $N_r$ are the linear drag coefficients, and $X_{u|u|}$, $Y_{v|v|}$, $Y_{r|r|}$, $N_{v|v|}$, and $N_{r|r|}$ are the nonlinear drag coefficients, respectively. The effective coupling coefficients $c_{vr}$, $c_{ur}$, and $c_{uv}$ combine the Coriolis and centripetal coupling of the rigid body and added mass with the lift acting on the hull, which grows with the forward speed and partly offsets that coupling \cite{fossen2011}. Without the lift, they reduce to $c_{vr} = m_{22}$, $c_{ur} = m_{11}$, and $c_{uv} = m_{22} - m_{11}$. Since the two contributions multiply the same products of velocities, only their sums can be identified from motion data.

By vectoring the propeller thrust through the steering angle of the drive, the control force and moment are expressed as
\begin{equation}
f_c = \begin{bmatrix} f_X \\ f_Y \\ f_N \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} F_g\cos\delta \\ F_g\sin\delta \\ -l_d F_g\sin\delta \end{bmatrix},
\label{eq_force}
\end{equation}
where $F_g$ is the propeller thrust at gear level $g \in \{\gR, \gN, \gF\}$ at the throttle setting used in this study, with $F_{\gN} = 0$ and $F_{\gR} < 0 < F_{\gF}$. Owing to the deadzone of Section~\ref{sec_dwell}, the thrust takes only these three values. Here, $\delta$ denotes the steering angle of the drive, and $l_d$ is the distance from the origin of the body fixed frame to the drive.

The steering angle and the steering rate are constrained by
\begin{equation}
|\delta| \le \delta_{\max}, \qquad |\dot{\delta}| \le \dot{\delta}_{\max},
\label{eq_steer}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_{\max}$ and $\dot{\delta}_{\max}$ denote the maximum steering angle and the maximum steering rate, respectively.

In addition, the disturbance $w$ is assumed to be unknown and to lie in the box
\begin{equation}
\calW = \bigl\{ w \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid \underline{w} \le w \le \bar{w} \bigr\},
\label{eq_wbox}
\end{equation}
where $\underline{w}$ and $\bar{w} = [\bar{w}_X, \bar{w}_Y, \bar{w}_N]^\top$ are the lower and upper bounds, respectively, and the inequality holds entry wise.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec_problem}

This section augments the state with the gear and its remaining lock time, defines the legal action set, and formulates the safety game.

\subsection{Augmented State and Legal Action Set}

Under the dwell, the admissible gear command depends on the time elapsed since the last shift. At the same physical state, any gear can be chosen if the last shift occurred long ago, whereas the gear is locked if a shift has just occurred. Since the physical state alone cannot distinguish the two cases, the process is not Markov. Therefore, the gear state and its remaining lock time are augmented to the state.

Let $(\tilde{x}, \tilde{y}) = (x - x_d, y - y_d)$ be the position error from the setpoint $(x_d, y_d)$, $\tilde{\psi}$ the heading relative to a fixed reference, and $\tau_g \in \{0, 1, \ldots, D_g - 1\}$ the remaining lock time in control steps. The augmented state is $\xi = (\tilde{x}, \tilde{y}, \tilde{\psi}, u, v, r, \delta, g, \tau_g) \in \Xi$, and the control is $c = (\dot{\delta}_c, g_c)$, where $\dot{\delta}_c \in \calA_\delta = [-\dot{\delta}_{\max}, \dot{\delta}_{\max}]$ and $g_c$ are the steering rate command and the gear command, respectively. The steering rate is used as the input because the maximizing control of the safety game tends to be bang bang, and a bang bang steering angle command cannot be followed by the steering actuator. The steering angle is then integrated over each control period and saturated at $\pm\delta_{\max}$.

The legal action set is given by
\begin{equation}
\calC(\xi) = \calA_\delta \times \calC_g(g, \tau_g), \qquad
\calC_g = \begin{cases} \{g\}, & \tau_g > 0,\\ \calN(g), & \tau_g = 0, \end{cases}
\label{eq_legal}
\end{equation}
where $\calN(g)$ is the set of $g$ and its adjacent levels in the order $(\gR, \gN, \gF)$, such as $\calN(\gR) = \{\gR, \gN\}$. Since the gear actuator cannot reverse directly, a reversal between forward and reverse passes through neutral, while the steering channel has no dwell constraint. The timer resets to $D_g - 1$ whenever the gear switches to a new level and otherwise counts down toward zero, where it stays while the gear is held. Hence, after the dwell, the gear can be held for any number of further steps and shifted at any later step. Since holding the current gear is always legal, $\calC(\xi)$ is nonempty. The augmented transition $\xi' = \Phi(\xi, c, w)$ integrates \eqref{eq_model} over one control period under the steering limits \eqref{eq_steer} and the legal gear and updates the timer. A policy $\pi \colon \Xi \to \calA_\delta \times \{\gR, \gN, \gF\}$ is dwell constrained admissible if $\pi(\xi) \in \calC(\xi)$ for every $\xi \in \Xi$, and the set of such policies is denoted by $\Pi_{\calC}$.


\subsection{Augmented Safety Game}\label{sec_game}

The task is encoded by the keep in function $h(\xi) = R - \sqrt{\tilde{x}^2 + \tilde{y}^2}$, whose zero superlevel set is the disk of radius $R$ around the setpoint. Since $h$ depends only on the position error, the heading is free and the ASV can weathervane. For an initial augmented state $\xi \in \Xi$, the safety value function is defined as
\begin{equation}
V_h^{*}(\xi) = \max_{\pi \in \Pi_{\calC}} \min_{\mu} \min_{k \ge 0} h(\xi_k),
\label{eq_value}
\end{equation}
where $\xi_k$ is the state at step $k$ of the trajectory that starts from $\xi_0 = \xi$ and evolves by $\xi_{k+1} = \Phi(\xi_k, \pi(\xi_k), w_k)$, and $\mu$ ranges over nonanticipative disturbance strategies that select $w_k \in \calW$ \cite{bansal2017}. Therefore, $V_h^{*}(\xi)$ is the largest worst case minimum of $h$ that a dwell constrained admissible policy can guarantee from $\xi$, and its zero superlevel set $\{\xi \mid V_h^{*}(\xi) \ge 0\}$ is the largest set from which the ASV can be kept inside the disk for all time.

By the dynamic programming principle \cite{li2023cdc}, $V_h^{*}$ satisfies the Bellman equation
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V_h^{*}(\xi) = \min\Bigl\{ h(\xi),\ &\max_{g_c \in \calC_g(g, \tau_g)} \max_{\dot{\delta}_c \in \calA_\delta} \\
&\min_{w \in \calW} V_h^{*}\bigl(\Phi(\xi, (\dot{\delta}_c, g_c), w)\bigr) \Bigr\},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq_bellman}
\end{equation}
where the dwell enters only through the legal gear set $\calC_g(g, \tau_g)$ and the timer update of $\Phi$.

\section{Dwell Constrained Safe Controller}\label{sec_method}

This section presents the learning of the dwell constrained safety value and the onboard safety policy.

The safety value is learned by an adversarial actor critic scheme that follows reachability constrained reinforcement learning \cite{yu2022} and its disturbed extension \cite{li2023cdc}. Neural network critics are regressed onto bootstrapped targets, while a protagonist maximizes and an adversary minimizes the critic.

Instead of a one step backup of \eqref{eq_bellman}, the critic target unrolls the simulator over $n \ge D_g$ steps. With a one step backup, the minimum of $h$ over a lock reaches the state where the lock begins only through the learned values at the intermediate locked states, so their approximation errors accumulate, and an overestimate certifies an unsafe locked state as safe. This occurred in preliminary training for locked states near the boundary of the constraint set $\{h \ge 0\}$. The unroll contains the entire lock that the first action can start, so $h$ over the lock is computed from the simulated states and the learned value is evaluated only at the end. Since \eqref{eq_legal} is enforced at every step, the gear in the unroll follows the same lock and hold rules as the plant.

The value is computed by repeatedly applying an update of the form \eqref{eq_bellman}, which is not guaranteed to converge to a unique solution without a discount. A discount $\gamma_h \in (0,1)$ is therefore introduced \cite{fisac2019}, as extended to the disturbed game in \cite{li2023cdc}, which guarantees this convergence. For a bounded function $U \colon \Xi \to \mathbb{R}$, the discounted one step operator $\calT_h$, which maps $U$ to its one step update, is defined as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
(\calT_h U)(\xi) &= (1-\gamma_h) h(\xi) + \gamma_h \min\Bigl\{ h(\xi),\ \max_{g_c \in \calC_g(g, \tau_g)} \\
&\qquad \max_{\dot{\delta}_c \in \calA_\delta} \min_{w \in \calW} U\bigl(\Phi(\xi, (\dot{\delta}_c, g_c), w)\bigr) \Bigr\},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq_onestep}
\end{equation}
and the $n$ step operator is its $n$ fold composition
\begin{equation}
\calT_h^{n} U = \underbrace{\calT_h \circ \cdots \circ \calT_h}_{n\ \text{times}}\, U,
\label{eq_nstep}
\end{equation}
in which the legal gear is chosen at every step. The discounted safety value $V_h$ is the fixed point of $\calT_h$, and it is obtained from any bounded $U$ as
\begin{equation}
V_h = \calT_h V_h = \calT_h^{n} V_h = \lim_{k \to \infty} \calT_h^{k}\, U,
\label{eq_fixed}
\end{equation}
where the convergence follows from Lemma~\ref{lem_aug}. {\color{red}The maximum over the steering rate command in \eqref{eq_onestep}, also with $\gamma_h = 1$, is assumed to be attained, which is standard for compact action sets and continuous dynamics \cite{bertsekas1972reach,akametalu2024minimum}.}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem_aug}
Let $h$ be bounded. Then $\calT_h$ is a monotone $\gamma_h$ contraction and $\calT_h^{n}$ is a monotone $\gamma_h^{n}$ contraction in the supremum norm, and both admit the same unique bounded fixed point $V_h$ with $V_h \le h$. Moreover, $\calS^{*} = \{\xi \mid V_h^{*}(\xi) \ge 0\}$ is a robust invariant set of the augmented system under dwell constrained admissible policies.
\end{lemma}

The proof is given in the Appendix. As $\gamma_h$ approaches one, $V_h$ is expected to approach $V_h^{*}$ \cite{li2023cdc}. A critic with residual $\varepsilon$ under a $\beta$ contraction lies within $\varepsilon/(1-\beta)$ of its fixed point, so the unroll target with modulus $\gamma_h^{n}$ in place of $\gamma_h$ reduces this bound by the factor $\bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\gamma_h^{j}\bigr)^{-1}$.

The fixed point of \eqref{eq_onestep} is learned as summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg_ris}. Every network receives the augmented state with the heading encoded by its sine and cosine and the gear by a one hot vector.

\subsubsection{Safety Critics}
Two safety critics $Q_{h,1}(\xi, c, w)$ and $Q_{h,2}(\xi, c, w)$ with target copies $\bar{Q}_{h,1}$ and $\bar{Q}_{h,2}$ approximate the value of applying the control $c = (\dot{\delta}_c, g_c)$ and the disturbance $w$ at $\xi$, and their minimum is used wherever a critic value is needed, which counteracts the overestimation that the maximizing protagonist tends to exploit \cite{fujimoto2018td3}. The regression target is the backward fold of \eqref{eq_nstep} along a simulator unroll of $n \ge D_g$ steps from a sampled state $\xi_0$. The committed action is applied at the first step, the greedy legal gear \eqref{eq_gear} and the protagonist $\pi_h$ act at every later step, and the adversary $\mu$ acts at every step. Let $\bar V(\xi) = \max_{g_c \in \calC_g(g, \tau_g)} \min_i \bar{Q}_{h,i}(\xi, (\pi_h(\xi), g_c), \mu(\xi))$ denote the learned estimate of $V_h$ at $\xi$ given by the target copies. Starting from this estimate at the last unrolled state, the regression target $y$ of the critics at the sampled state and the committed action is computed by the backward recursion
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
y_n &= \bar V(\xi_n), \qquad y = y_0, \\
y_j &= (1-\gamma_h) h(\xi_j) + \gamma_h \min\{ h(\xi_j),\ y_{j+1} \},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq_target}
\end{equation}
for $j = n-1, \ldots, 0$, where $\xi_j$ is the unrolled state at step $j$ and $y_j$ is the value of the unroll from step $j$ onward. The target $y$ thus evaluates \eqref{eq_nstep} along the unroll with $U$ given by $\bar V$ and with $\pi_h$, $g^\star$, and $\mu$ in place of the maximization and minimization, so each regression approximates one iteration of \eqref{eq_fixed}. The bootstrap term is clipped to $[-h_{\mathrm{floor}}, R]$ and $h$ is floored at $-h_{\mathrm{floor}}$ outside the disk, which keeps the value bounded as required by Lemma~\ref{lem_aug}. Each critic is trained to minimize its squared error to $y$, and each target copy follows its critic slowly by the soft update in Algorithm~\ref{alg_ris}, so that the target changes gradually.

\subsubsection{Protagonist and Adversary}
The protagonist $\pi_h$ and the adversary $\mu$ are deterministic policies whose outputs are scaled by $\tanh$ into $\calA_\delta$ and the box $\calW$, respectively. Let $q(\xi, c) = \min_i Q_{h,i}(\xi, c, \mu(\xi))$ denote the minimum critic at the adversary disturbance. The greedy legal gear is selected as
\begin{equation}
g^\star(\xi) = \arg\max_{g_c \in \calC_g(g, \tau_g)} q\bigl(\xi, (\pi_h(\xi), g_c)\bigr).
\label{eq_gear}
\end{equation}
Both policies are trained by deterministic policy gradient, where the protagonist maximizes
\begin{equation}
J_\pi = \mathbb{E}_{\xi}\Bigl[ q\bigl(\xi, (\pi_h(\xi), g^\star(\xi))\bigr) \Bigr],
\label{eq_actor}
\end{equation}
and the adversary minimizes $\min_i Q_{h,i}$ at the action $(\pi_h(\xi), g^\star(\xi))$.

\subsubsection{Staged Training and Coverage}
Training starts from networks trained on the previously identified model of the ASV with a box of 0.9 times its bounds. On the present model, two stages use the box at 0.9 times $\calW$ and then at $\calW$, each ramping the scale $\kappa$ from the previous box over its first updates. In every stage the protagonist and the adversary are updated in alternating blocks until the last four evaluations of the certified fraction agree within a small tolerance, then the adversary alone, and then the critics alone. After the last stage the critics alone were calibrated once more with a smaller learning rate and more parallel simulators, since the value correlation of the audit fell short of its acceptance criterion before this step. The discount is fixed at its value in Table~\ref{tab_params}, since a lower discount counts late and shallow departures from the disk as safe. Every update samples $N$ augmented states over $\Xi$, including the gear, the timer, and states in the middle of a lock, adds them to a buffer of recent states, and trains on a batch resampled from the buffer, so that hard states near the boundary are revisited.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Adversarial Learning of the Dwell Constrained Safety Value}
\label{alg_ris}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Initialize critics $Q_{h,1}, Q_{h,2}$, targets $\bar{Q}_{h,1}, \bar{Q}_{h,2}$, protagonist $\pi_h$, and adversary $\mu$ from the networks of the previous stage, and set the disturbance scale $\kappa$ to the ratio of the previous box to the current one
\FOR{each update}
\STATE Sample $N$ augmented states over $\Xi$, including $g$ and $\tau_g$, add them to the buffer, and restore the $N$ parallel simulators to a batch resampled from the buffer
\STATE At each sampled state, choose a random legal committed gear and perturb the steering command and the disturbance $\kappa\,\mu(\xi)$ by exploration noise
\STATE Unroll each simulator for $n$ steps, with the committed gear at the first step and the greedy legal gear \eqref{eq_gear} at every later step
\STATE Form $y$ by the backward fold \eqref{eq_target} with clipping
\STATE Update $Q_{h,1}, Q_{h,2}$ by regression onto $y$
\STATE Update $\pi_h$ by ascending \eqref{eq_actor}
\STATE Update $\mu$ by descending \eqref{eq_actor}
\STATE Soft update $\bar{Q}_{h,i} \gets (1-\rho)\bar{Q}_{h,i} + \rho Q_{h,i}$
\STATE Advance $\kappa$ toward one
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Certified Set}
After training, the certified value is $\hat V_h(\xi) = q(\xi, (\pi_h(\xi), g^\star(\xi)))$ and the certified set is $\hat{\calS} = \{\xi \mid \hat V_h(\xi) \ge 0\}$. As in other learned reachability methods, the approximation error of the networks is not bounded, the learned adversary may be weaker than the worst case disturbance, and the discounted fixed point is optimistic relative to \eqref{eq_value} for $\gamma_h < 1$ \cite{hsu2023sim2lab,li2025certifiable}. Prior work therefore calibrates the certified level after training, by a margin from the failure set \cite{hsu2023sim2lab} or by a high confidence bound from sampled rollouts \cite{borquez2024filtering}. Following this practice, the certificate is accepted on the level set $\hat{\calS}_\tau = \{\xi \mid \hat V_h(\xi) \ge \tau\}$ with the smallest margin $\tau \ge 0$ at which the Monte Carlo audit of Section~\ref{sec_sim} gives a hit rate of at least 95 percent and a correlation of at least 0.5 between the certified value and the realized margin. Since a longer remaining lock only removes legal gear sequences, $V_h$ is nonincreasing in $\tau_g$, so for each gear the slice of the certified set over the physical state shrinks as the remaining lock time grows.

\subsubsection{Onboard Safety Controller}

The deployed controller applies $\pi_h(\xi)$ and $g^\star(\xi)$ of \eqref{eq_gear} at each control period without a separate task policy, so a shift is requested only when it is legal and raises the certified value. Since this policy maximizes the certified margin, it keeps the ASV away from the boundary of the disk.

As baselines, the dwell unaware \risnd, a proportional derivative controller, denoted \pid, and a CIA based MPC, denoted \ciampc, were implemented. \risnd is initialized from the networks of \dcris with the timer inputs removed and trained at $\calW$ on the plant without the dwell, where it passes the audit at every margin. \pid is the dynamic positioning PID controller of \cite{fossen2011}, whose shift requests are held by the plant during locks and whose gains were tuned on an earlier identified model of the ASV. \ciampc is the CIA based MPC of \cite{chenlazar2022} with the rounding of \cite{buerger2020pycombina,sager2011}, which predicts with the identified model under the six step gear hold and without a disturbance model. \pid and \ciampc are also run in the field, while \risnd is evaluated only in simulation.

The strongest case is the ASV moving ahead in $\gF$, which cannot reverse until the lock ends, so the set of \dcris shrinks the most and keeps only the states behind the setpoint, from which the forward motion carries the ASV toward the setpoint.

All three controllers shifted the gear at a similar rate, so the improvement was not obtained by shifting more often, but the gear usage differed. \pid and \ciampc cycled through forward, neutral, and reverse, whereas \dcris mostly alternated between reverse and neutral and rarely engaged forward, as listed in Table~\ref{tab_field}. These results confirm that the safety policy trained in simulation transferred to the ASV without retraining and kept the ASV near the setpoint under real sea conditions.

The behavior during locked intervals is the direct field evidence for the dwell constrained formulation of Section~\ref{sec_method}. The PLC enforced the dwell for every controller, and the measured interval between consecutive shifts was never shorter than the dwell in any run. The mean growth of the distance within the lock after a shift was the smallest for \dcris, whereas its largest single growth was the largest among the three controllers, indicating that a lock opened at an unfavorable moment can still consume a large part of the margin. Under the dwell, a reversal between forward and reverse must pass through neutral and therefore spans at least two locks. \pid and \ciampc repeatedly committed to such full reversals, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig_field_compare}(f).

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec_conclusion}

This article presented a dwell constrained RIS framework for safe weathervaning control of an internal combustion sterndrive ASV. Since the dwell restricts only the control player, the safe set shrinks as the remaining lock time grows. With the gear state and remaining lock time augmented to the state, the safety value was learned by adversarial reinforcement learning with legality masking and a target unrolled over the dwell, and the learned safety policy ran onboard. Simulations, \pending{simulation headline to be stated once the simulation numbers are final}, and full scale field experiments validated the proposed framework, which kept the ASV closer to the setpoint than the baseline controllers with short excursions at sea. Future work will quantify the approximation error of the neural representation and extend the field validation beyond a single dwell channel.

\appendix[Proof of Lemma 1]

The operator \eqref{eq_onestep} is a monotone $\gamma_h$ contraction in the supremum norm by the argument of \cite[Theorem~2]{li2023cdc}, which builds on the discounted construction of \cite{fisac2019}. The instantaneous term $(1-\gamma_h)h$ is fixed, the outer minimum with $h$ is nonexpansive, the minimization over the compact box $\calW$ is nonexpansive, and the maximization over the nonempty legal set $\calC(\xi)$, a finite gear set times a compact interval, is nonexpansive, so for bounded $U$ and $U'$,
\begin{equation}
\|\calT_h U - \calT_h U'\|_\infty \le \gamma_h \|U - U'\|_\infty ,
\end{equation}
and each operation preserves the pointwise order. Therefore, $\calT_h^{n}$ is a monotone $\gamma_h^{n}$ contraction. By the Banach fixed point theorem on the complete space of bounded functions on $\Xi$, $\calT_h$ has a unique bounded fixed point $V_h$, and since $\calT_h^{n}$ is a contraction with $\calT_h^{n} V_h = V_h$, $V_h$ is also its unique fixed point. Since each application returns at most $h$, the fixed point satisfies $V_h \le h$.

For the invariance, $V_h^{*}$ satisfies \eqref{eq_bellman} by the dynamic programming principle. Let $V_h^{*}(\xi) \ge 0$. Then $h(\xi) \ge 0$, and by the attained maximum there is a legal control $c \in \calC(\xi)$ such that $V_h^{*}(\Phi(\xi, c, w)) \ge 0$ for every $w \in \calW$. Selecting such a control at every state defines a policy in $\Pi_{\calC}$ that keeps the state in $\calS^{*}$ for all time against every disturbance, which establishes the robust invariance of $\calS^{*}$ under dwell constrained admissible policies. $\blacksquare$
