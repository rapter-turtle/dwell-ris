\begin{abstract}
This article presents a dwell constrained robust invariant set (RIS) framework for the safe weathervaning station keeping of an autonomous surface vessel (ASV) driven by a single engine and a sterndrive. Station keeping with this propulsion system is challenging because each gear position is held for a minimum dwell time, and a lock in neutral removes both thrust and steering authority since the sterndrive steers by vectoring the thrust. Existing safety certificates do not consider this dwell, which can lead to an optimistic certificate that declares unsafe states safe. To address this problem, the proposed framework augments the gear state and its remaining lock time to the state and learns the dwell constrained safety value by a one step adversarial backup with legality masking. Since the dwell restricts only the control player of the safety game, the safe set becomes a family of sets that depends on the remaining lock time, on which a task policy and a value threshold shield are built. The proposed framework is validated through numerical simulations against a dwell unaware certificate, \pending{simulation headline to be stated once the simulation numbers are final}, and through full scale field experiments on an 8 m ASV, in which the proposed controller kept the ASV inside the keep in disk for a larger portion of the time and with shorter excursions than the PID and NMPC baselines.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec_intro}

\IEEEPARstart{S}{mall} autonomous surface vessels (ASVs) have been widely applied to hydrographic survey, water quality sampling, harbor inspection, and passenger transport in confined waters \cite{liu2016,shi2017,lee2024canal}. Many of these missions contain phases in which the ASV must hold a position rather than follow a path. Such operations include waiting at a survey station until a sensor cast is complete, waiting in front of a lock or a berth until the passage is cleared, and loitering around a rendezvous point until a partner arrives. In these phases, reliable station keeping is a fundamental capability, and the safety of the ASV depends on how well the position is maintained under wind and current.

However, an ASV without a lateral actuator cannot hold the position and the heading at the same time by any smooth static state feedback. The time varying or discontinuous laws that solve this nonholonomic stabilization problem require persistent surge and yaw action, resulting in energy consumption and actuator wear \cite{pettersen2000underactuated,pettersen2004guas,qu2022positioning}. Weathervaning is the practical alternative, in which the position is held inside a region around the setpoint while the bow turns against the mean environmental load, thereby minimizing the lateral load that the ASV cannot counter. This idea has been adopted in weather optimal positioning of dynamically positioned ships \cite{fossen2001wopc}, in a disturbance observer that points the bow against the estimated load \cite{kim2014weathervaning}, in wind feedforward for an underactuated ASV \cite{pereira2008stationkeeping}, and in adaptive heading assignment for underactuated USVs \cite{qu2022positioning}. Consequently, a small ASV with a single engine and a sterndrive performs station keeping most naturally by weathervaning.

However, the sterndrive that makes such an ASV inexpensive also makes the task difficult, and this difficulty arises from its actuation mechanism. The engine drives the propeller through a gearbox with reverse, neutral, and forward positions, and the actuation system enforces a minimum dwell of three seconds between consecutive shift commands for the reasons explained in Section~\ref{sec_dwell}. During the dwell the controller cannot change the sign of the thrust, and since a sterndrive steers by vectoring the propeller thrust, a lock in neutral leaves the ASV with neither thrust nor steering authority. Meanwhile, wind and current disturbances continue to act on the ASV during the lock, leading to a growing position error until the lock expires.

Station keeping controllers for marine craft have been widely studied, ranging from proportional integral derivative (PID) and line of sight designs \cite{yu2022los} to nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) with identified vessel models \cite{lee2024canal}. These controllers and the weathervaning controllers above assume a thrust that can be varied continuously and reversed at any instant. Moreover, they establish convergence or ultimate boundedness of the position error without certifying a region that the ASV will not leave under bounded disturbances. However, neither assumption holds for an ASV whose thrust direction is locked during a gear shift.

When a discrete actuator must respect a minimum dwell, the model predictive literature on switched systems offers mixed integer and dwell constrained formulations \cite{zhang2016,vicente2019,lavaei2021,zhuang2023,chenlazar2022}. However, these results are restricted to nominal or linear settings. Among them, the switched tube constructions of \cite{zhang2016,vicente2019,lavaei2021} tighten constraints against additive disturbances for linear dynamics only, and the persistent dwell results of \cite{zhuang2023} are stated for linear switched systems. The nonlinear scheme of \cite{chenlazar2022} establishes recursive feasibility through an $\ell$ step control invariant terminal set, but it has no tightening mechanism for disturbances. In addition, all of these works treat the dwell as a design output that guarantees stability or feasibility, whereas on a sterndrive the dwell is given by the actuation system to protect the drivetrain. Therefore, the region in which the ASV can remain safe under the given dwell must be computed.

Safety against bounded disturbances is naturally posed as a two player zero sum reachability game \cite{bansal2017}, and learning based methods have been extensively studied to compute its value for nonlinear systems beyond the reach of grid based solvers. Among them, the discounted safety backup of Fisac et al. \cite{fisac2019}, reachability constrained reinforcement learning \cite{yu2022}, its adversarial extension by Li et al. \cite{li2023cdc,li2309dual}, decomposition based safety filters \cite{li2311decomp}, and DeepReach \cite{bansal2021deepreach} all deliver a value function whose zero superlevel set is a robust invariant set (RIS). However, all of these methods assume that the full control set is available at every step, and none of them models an actuator that locks. The exact computation of disturbance invariant sets under a dwell time restriction by Dehghan and Ong \cite{dehghan2012} is the closest predecessor of the present work, but it is confined to linear polytopic dynamics and quantifies universally over disturbance sequences. In addition, it provides only a binary set membership, which cannot be used as a continuous safety margin for a shield. As a result, a safety certificate that explicitly accounts for a physical actuator dwell under disturbances and nonlinear dynamics has not been developed.

In this study, we develop a dwell constrained RIS framework for the safe weathervaning station keeping of an ASV by explicitly accounting for the gear shift dwell of the sterndrive. Since the dwell restricts only the control input of the zero sum safety game while the disturbance acts at every step, the safe set becomes a family of sets that depends on the gear state and its remaining lock time. Unlike the single sets of \cite{dehghan2012,zhang2016,vicente2019} and the dwell free certificates of \cite{fisac2019,li2023cdc}, the proposed framework augments the gear state and its remaining lock time to the state. The dwell constrained safety value is then learned by a one step adversarial backup in which legality is enforced both in the plant and in the backup. Based on the learned value, a task policy and a safety shield are constructed, enabling the task policy to operate inside the certified set, and the overall framework is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig_framework}.

The proposed framework was implemented on an 8 m sterndrive ASV and validated in simulation against a dwell unaware certificate and in full scale field experiments against PID and NMPC baselines. To the best of our knowledge, this study represents the first field demonstration of a learned robust safety certificate on a full scale ASV subject to a physical actuator dwell. The main contributions of this article are summarized as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A dwell constrained RIS is formulated for weathervaning station keeping, in which the gear state and its remaining lock time are augmented to the state.
\item A one step adversarial learning scheme with legality masking is developed for the dwell constrained safety value, together with a task policy and a value threshold shield.
\item The proposed framework is validated through simulations against a dwell unaware certificate and through full scale field experiments on an 8 m sterndrive ASV against PID and NMPC baselines.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Description and Modeling}\label{sec_system}

This section describes the ASV and its actuation system, the origin of the gear shift dwell, and the vehicle dynamics model used for training together with the disturbance model. In the following, the modeling practice of \cite{lee2024canal} and the model class of \cite{fossen2011} are adopted.

\subsection{Vessel and Actuation System}\label{sec_vessel}

The experimental platform is a Neo AURA 240 EXP with a deep V planing hull, propelled by a single inboard diesel engine \pending{model} through a MerCruiser Bravo~1 sterndrive, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig_vessel}(a). On the ASV, the GPS antenna is mounted on the T top, while the attitude and heading reference system (AHRS) and the control PC, a laptop computer running ROS~2, are located at the console. Table~\ref{tab_boat} lists the specifications of the platform and its sensors.

The controller accesses three actuation channels through the chain shown in Fig.~\ref{fig_vessel}(b). Among them, the engine speed is commanded over a CAN interface, while two electric linear servo cylinders execute the gear shift among reverse, neutral, and forward and the steering angle of the drive. The two cylinders act on the shift cable and the drive pivot, respectively, and both are driven by an LS XGB programmable logic controller (PLC) through an EtherCAT servo interface. A bow thruster is installed on the vessel but is not used in this study, so the results concern a single engine sterndrive configuration.

\subsection{Gear Shift Dwell}\label{sec_dwell}

A shift between forward and reverse passes through neutral, and the clutch of the sterndrive gearbox must engage at low engine speed to avoid shock loading of the gear set. Therefore, each shift requires a finite mechanical time of \pending{mechanical shift time}, and an immediate reversal of a shift that has just been completed stresses the drivetrain. To protect the drivetrain, the minimum interval between consecutive shift commands was set to 3~s on the ASV of this study by adding a safety margin to the mechanical shift time. The PLC explicitly enforces this interval, ensuring that the drivetrain is protected regardless of the controller. Consequently, the dwell is given by the actuation system and cannot be shortened by the controller.

With the control period of 0.5~s, the dwell corresponds to $D_g = 6$ control steps. A shift request issued during the dwell is discarded by the PLC, and the current gear is maintained. Therefore, the controller must account for the time at which a shift becomes available.

\subsection{Vehicle Dynamics Modeling}\label{sec_model}

The three degree of freedom horizontal plane model of \cite{fossen2011} describes the ASV in the body fixed and inertial frames of Fig.~\ref{fig_frames}. Following \cite{lee2024canal}, the model is formulated as
\begin{subequations}\label{eq_model}
\begin{align}
M\dot{\nu} + C(\nu)\nu + D(\nu)\nu &= \bm{f}_c + M d, \\
\dot{\eta} &= R(\psi)\nu, \\
R(\psi) &= \begin{bmatrix} \cos\psi & -\sin\psi & 0 \\ \sin\psi & \cos\psi & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix},
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\nu = [u, v, r]^\top$ and $\eta = [x, y, \psi]^\top$ are the velocity and position vectors, respectively, and $M$, $C(\nu)$, and $D(\nu)$ denote the inertia matrix, the Coriolis and centripetal matrix, and the damping matrix. Here, $\bm{f}_c = [f_X, f_Y, f_N]^\top$ represents the control forces and moment in each direction, $d = [d_u, d_v, d_r]^\top$ is the disturbance acceleration bounded in \eqref{eq_wbox}, and $R(\psi)$ is the rotation matrix from the body fixed frame to the inertial frame. In this article, $\bm{f}_c$ denotes the control vector because $\tau$ is reserved for the shield margin and $\tau_g$ for the remaining lock time.

Assuming that the ASV is symmetric in the $x$ and $y$ directions, $M$, $C(\nu)$, and $D(\nu)$ can be expressed as
\begin{equation}
M = \operatorname{diag}(m_{11}, m_{22}, m_{33}),
\label{eq_mass}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
C(\nu) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & -m_{22} v \\ 0 & 0 & m_{11} u \\ m_{22} v & -m_{11} u & 0 \end{bmatrix},
\label{eq_coriolis}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
D(\nu) = -\begin{bmatrix} X_u & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & Y_v & Y_r \\ 0 & N_v & N_r \end{bmatrix}
- \begin{bmatrix} X_{u|u|}|u| & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & Y_{v|v|}|v| & Y_{r|r|}|r| \\ 0 & N_{v|v|}|v| & N_{r|r|}|r| \end{bmatrix},
\label{eq_damping}
\end{equation}
where $m_{11}$, $m_{22}$, and $m_{33}$ are the mass and moment of inertia including the added mass and added moment of inertia. The coefficients $X_u$, $Y_v$, $Y_r$, $N_v$, and $N_r$ are the linear drag coefficients, and $X_{u|u|}$, $Y_{v|v|}$, $Y_{r|r|}$, $N_{v|v|}$, and $N_{r|r|}$ are the nonlinear drag coefficients, respectively.

By vectoring the propeller thrust through the steering angle of the drive, the control force and moment are expressed as
\begin{equation}
\bm{f}_c = \begin{bmatrix} f_X \\ f_Y \\ f_N \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} F_g\cos\delta \\ F_g\sin\delta \\ -l_d F_g\sin\delta \end{bmatrix},
\label{eq_force}
\end{equation}
where $F_g$ is the propeller thrust at gear level $g \in \{\gR, \gN, \gF\}$ at the throttle setting used in this study, with $F_{\gN} = 0$ and $F_{\gR} < 0 < F_{\gF}$. Here, $\delta$ denotes the steering angle of the drive, and $l_d$ is the distance from the origin of the body fixed frame to the drive.

In \cite{lee2024canal} the outboard thrust is commanded continuously through the throttle as $F = c\,n_T^2$, whereas the thrust in this study takes only the three values selected by the gear, and the gear is the channel that carries the dwell. Since the lateral force $f_Y$ and the yaw moment $f_N$ are proportional to $F_g$, both vanish together with the thrust in neutral.

The steering angle moves toward its command $\delta_c$ at no more than the slew rate of the servo cylinder. Over an integration substep $\Delta t_s$, the steering update can be expressed as
\begin{equation}
\delta^{+} = \delta + \operatorname{sat}_{\dot{\delta}_{\max}\Delta t_s}\!\bigl(\delta_c - \delta\bigr), \qquad |\delta_c| \le \delta_{\max},
\label{eq_steer}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_{\max}$ and $\dot{\delta}_{\max}$ are the angle limit and the rate limit of the servo cylinder, respectively \pending{values}.

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\IfFileExists{figures/fig_frames.pdf}{\includegraphics[width=0.6\linewidth]{figures/fig_frames.pdf}}{\figph{Fig. 3 placeholder. Coordinate frames and sterndrive geometry.}{4cm}}
\caption{Coordinate frames and sterndrive geometry. Inertial and body fixed frames, the keep in disk of radius $R$ around the setpoint, and the thrust $F_g$ vectored by the steering angle $\delta$ at the distance $l_d$ behind the origin.}
\label{fig_frames}
\end{figure}

A body frame acceleration $d = [d_u, d_v, d_r]^\top$ represents the environmental load, enters \eqref{eq_model} through the term $Md$, and is bounded by the box
\begin{equation}
\calW = [-\bar{d}_u, \bar{d}_u] \times [-\bar{d}_v, \bar{d}_v] \times [-\bar{d}_r, \bar{d}_r].
\label{eq_wbox}
\end{equation}
Based on drift data recorded on the ASV with the engine at idle and the gear in neutral, the bounds cover the recorded drift with a margin \pending{bounds $\bar d_u$, $\bar d_v$, $\bar d_r$ and the recording conditions}. In this condition, the measured accelerations reflect the wind and current load alone. No assumption is made on the time behavior of $d$ within $\calW$, since the disturbance is chosen by an adversary at every step in the safety game of Section~\ref{sec_problem}, providing a stronger model than any particular wind spectrum.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec_problem}

This section augments the gear state and its remaining lock time to the state, defines the legal action set induced by the dwell, and shows that the augmented game retains the structure required by the discounted safety backup.

\subsection{Markov Property Under Gear Shift Dwell}

Under the dwell, two identical physical states can admit different gear commands. If the gear was shifted three seconds ago, any gear command is admissible, whereas if it was shifted half a second ago, no gear command is admissible for the next two and a half seconds. Since the admissible gear command depends on the time elapsed since the last shift, the process is not Markov in the physical state alone. A value function learned on the physical state therefore conflates the two situations. To restore the Markov property, the gear state and its remaining lock time are augmented to the state.

\subsection{Augmented State and Legal Action Set}

Let $(\tilde{x}, \tilde{y}) = (x - x_d, y - y_d)$ be the position error from the setpoint $(x_d, y_d)$, and let $\tilde{\psi}$ be the heading relative to a fixed reference. The heading is kept in the state for the dynamics but is unconstrained by the task. Let $\tau_g \in \{0, 1, \ldots, D_g - 1\}$ denote the remaining lock time of the shift in control steps, where $\tau_g = 0$ means that the gear may be shifted now. The augmented state is defined as
\begin{equation}
\xi = (\tilde{x}, \tilde{y}, \tilde{\psi}, u, v, r, \delta, g, \tau_g) \in \Xi,
\label{eq_xi}
\end{equation}
and the control is $c = (\delta_c, g_c)$, where $\delta_c \in \calA_\delta = [-\delta_{\max}, \delta_{\max}]$ and $g_c$ are the steering command and the gear command, respectively.

The legal action set is given by
\begin{equation}
C(\xi) = \calA_\delta \times C_g(g, \tau_g), \qquad
C_g = \begin{cases} \{g\}, & \tau_g > 0,\\ \{\gR, \gN, \gF\}, & \tau_g = 0, \end{cases}
\label{eq_legal}
\end{equation}
where the steering channel has no dwell constraint, while the gear channel is locked whenever $\tau_g > 0$. Whenever the gear switches to a new level, the timer resets to $D_g - 1$, and otherwise it counts down toward zero. Holding the current gear is always legal, ensuring that $C(\xi)$ is nonempty, and it is compact as the product of a finite set and a compact interval. The augmented transition $\xi' = F(\xi, c, d)$ consists of the physical map obtained by integrating \eqref{eq_model} and \eqref{eq_steer} over one control period under the legal gear, together with the timer update.

\begin{definition}[Dwell Constrained Admissible Policy]\label{def_admissible}
A policy $\pi \colon \Xi \to \calA_\delta \times \{\gR, \gN, \gF\}$ is dwell constrained admissible if $\pi(\xi) \in C(\xi)$ for every $\xi \in \Xi$. The set of all such policies is denoted by $\Pi_C$.
\end{definition}

Here, the dwell is a given constraint of the actuation system of Section~\ref{sec_dwell} and is not a design variable of the controller, and the object computed in this article is the safe set under that given dwell. Both the PLC, which discards a shift request issued during a lock, and the legal action set $C(\xi)$ effectively enforce the dwell, so every admissible trajectory satisfies it by construction.

\begin{remark}\label{rem_masking}
The legality structure \eqref{eq_legal} must be enforced both in the plant and in the backup of Section~\ref{sec_game}. In the plant, a shift request issued during a lock is ignored and the current gear is held, as the PLC does on the ASV, while in the backup the maximization ranges over $C(\xi)$ instead of the full action set. If the legality is enforced only in the plant, the neural network must learn the restriction from data. In this case, the approximation error can lead the learned value to assume that a locked gear can be shifted immediately, resulting in an optimistic certificate that declares unsafe states safe, and this failure is examined in Section~\ref{sec_sim_a1}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Augmented Safety Game}\label{sec_game}

The task is encoded by the keep in function defined as
\begin{equation}
h(\xi) = R - \sqrt{\tilde{x}^2 + \tilde{y}^2},
\label{eq_h}
\end{equation}
whose zero superlevel set is the disk of radius $R$ around the setpoint. Since $h$ depends only on the position error, the heading is free and the ASV can weathervane. On the augmented state, the safety value function is defined as
\begin{equation}
V_h^{*}(\xi) = \max_{\pi \in \Pi_C} \min_{\mu} \min_{k \ge 0} h(\xi_k),
\label{eq_value}
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ ranges over nonanticipative disturbance strategies with values in $\calW$ \cite{bansal2017}. This value is the best worst case margin achievable from $\xi$ under the legally restricted control, and $\{\xi \mid V_h^{*}(\xi) \ge 0\}$ is the largest set on which the ASV can remain inside the disk forever against every admissible disturbance.

However, the undiscounted self consistency condition of \eqref{eq_value} is not a contraction. Fisac et al. \cite{fisac2019} introduced a discount $\gamma_h \in (0,1)$ that restores the contraction, and this device was extended to the disturbed game by Li et al. \cite{li2023cdc}. On the augmented state, the resulting backup is given by
\begin{equation}
(\calT_h V)(\xi) = (1-\gamma_h) h(\xi) + \gamma_h \min\Bigl\{ h(\xi),\ \max_{c \in C(\xi)} \min_{d \in \calW} V\bigl(F(\xi, c, d)\bigr) \Bigr\},
\label{eq_backup}
\end{equation}
which differs from the operator of \cite{li2023cdc} only in that the maximization ranges over the state dependent legal set. The following lemma shows that the augmented backup preserves the contraction property of \cite{li2023cdc}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem_aug}
Let the augmented dynamics follow \eqref{eq_xi} and \eqref{eq_legal}, let $h$ be bounded, and let $\calT_h$ be defined by \eqref{eq_backup}. Then $\calT_h$ is a monotone $\gamma_h$ contraction in the supremum norm, it admits a unique bounded fixed point $V_h$, and the set $\calS = \{\xi \mid V_h(\xi) \ge 0\}$ is a robust invariant set of the augmented system under dwell constrained admissible policies.
\end{lemma}

The proof is given in the Appendix. Lemma~\ref{lem_aug} indicates that the state dependent legal action set does not affect the contraction property, since the proof requires only that the admissible action set is nonempty and compact at every state. As a result, the effect of the dwell appears in the shape of the fixed point of the backup, and this shape is examined in Section~\ref{sec_method}.

\begin{remark}\label{rem_channels}
The formulation extends to $N$ independent switching channels, each with its own levels, dwell, and timer, by taking $C(\xi)$ as the product of the per channel legal sets. Lemma~\ref{lem_aug} holds without modification since no step of its proof counts the channels, and only the gear channel is treated in this article.
\end{remark}

\section{Dwell Constrained Safe Controller}\label{sec_method}

This section presents the proposed dwell constrained safe controller. We first show that the safe set under the dwell is a family of sets that depends on the gear state $(g, \tau_g)$ and locate its physical origin in the asymmetry between the control and the disturbance on a sterndrive. We then describe the one step learning of the dwell constrained safety value and the task policy and shield built on it.

\subsection{Safe Set Family Under Remaining Lock Time}\label{sec_family}

For fixed discrete coordinates of the augmented state, the slice of the RIS over the physical coordinates is defined as
\begin{equation}
\calS(g, \tau_g) = \bigl\{ (\tilde{x}, \tilde{y}, \tilde{\psi}, u, v, r, \delta) \mid V_h(\xi) \ge 0 \bigr\}.
\label{eq_family}
\end{equation}
Each choice of gear and timer produces a different slice, so the safe set is a family of sets that depends on the gear state $(g, \tau_g)$. When $\tau_g = 0$ the gear can react immediately and the slice is large. In contrast, when $\tau_g = D_g - 1$ the ASV must survive the entire lock without gear authority, so the margin that the disturbance can erode during the lock must be reserved in advance and the slice is small. As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig_family}, the slice decreases as the remaining lock time increases, or equivalently grows with the elapsed dwell $D_g - 1 - \tau_g$.

The physical origin of the family is an asymmetry in the game \eqref{eq_value}, in which the dwell binds only the maximizing player. On a sterndrive this asymmetry is particularly severe because the steering vectors the thrust. During a lock in neutral the propeller produces no thrust, and by \eqref{eq_force} no lateral force and no yaw moment either, so the control authority vanishes for up to three seconds. During a lock in forward or reverse the ASV can steer but cannot reverse its thrust. In this case, an ASV that has just engaged forward to return toward the setpoint cannot brake if the wind pushes it across the setpoint. Throughout the lock the disturbance acts freely at every step.

Restricting the admissible actions of the control player reduces the value of the zero sum game, resulting in a smaller safe set. Consequently, the adversary of \eqref{eq_value} does not require a larger disturbance, and the most critical disturbance acts immediately after the gear is locked at an unfavorable level. Since the remaining lock time is part of its argument, the dwell constrained safety value accurately anticipates this timing, whereas a dwell unaware value cannot.

\subsection{One Step Learning of the Dwell Constrained Safety Value}\label{sec_learning}

An actor critic scheme with a one step adversarial backup learns the fixed point of \eqref{eq_backup}, as summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg_ris}. The scheme follows reachability constrained reinforcement learning \cite{yu2022} and its disturbed extension \cite{li2023cdc} and is adapted to the legality structure. As the network input, the augmented state is encoded as
\begin{equation}
\bigl(\tilde{x}/R,\ \tilde{y}/R,\ \sin\tilde{\psi},\ \cos\tilde{\psi},\ u,\ v,\ r,\ \delta/\delta_{\max},\ \operatorname{onehot}(g),\ \tau_g/D_g\bigr),
\label{eq_input}
\end{equation}
enabling every network to observe the gear and the timer.

\subsubsection{Safety Critics}
Two safety critics $Q_{h,1}(\xi, c, d)$ and $Q_{h,2}(\xi, c, d)$ with target copies $\bar{Q}_{h,1}$ and $\bar{Q}_{h,2}$ approximate the value of the joint action and disturbance, and the minimum of the two is used wherever a critic value is needed. For a transition $(\xi, c, d, \xi')$ drawn from the replay buffer, the regression target is formulated as
\begin{equation}
y = (1-\gamma_h) h(\xi) + \gamma_h \min\Bigl\{ h(\xi),\ \min_{i} \bar{Q}_{h,i}\bigl(\xi', \pi_h(\xi'), \mu(\xi')\bigr) \Bigr\},
\label{eq_target}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_h$ and $\mu$ are the protagonist and the adversary described below, respectively. Since $h$ is bounded above by $R$, the bootstrap term is clipped to the physical value range $[-h_{\mathrm{floor}}, R]$, and $h$ itself is floored at $-h_{\mathrm{floor}}$ outside the disk, ensuring that the value remains bounded as required by Lemma~\ref{lem_aug}. A squared loss regresses the critics onto $y$, and the target copies follow the critics by a soft update.

\subsubsection{Protagonist}
The protagonist $\pi_h$ outputs a Gaussian steering command squashed by $\tanh$ into $\calA_\delta$ and sampled by the reparameterization trick, together with a categorical distribution over the gear levels in which the illegal levels outside $C_g(g, \tau_g)$ are masked to zero probability. Let $q(\xi, c) = \min_i Q_{h,i}(\xi, c, \mu(\xi))$ denote the minimum critic at the disturbance of the adversary. The protagonist is trained to maximize the exact expectation of $q$ over the legal discrete actions, which is formulated as
\begin{equation}
J_\pi = \mathbb{E}_{\xi}\Bigl[ \sum_{g' \in C_g(g,\tau_g)} p_\pi(g' \mid \xi)\ \mathbb{E}_{\delta' \sim \pi_h(\cdot \mid \xi)}\bigl[ q\bigl(\xi, (\delta', g')\bigr) \bigr] \Bigr],
\label{eq_actor}
\end{equation}
where the at most three legal gear levels are enumerated rather than sampled, thereby removing the variance of a sampled discrete action.

\subsubsection{Adversary}
The adversary $\mu(\xi)$ is a deterministic policy whose output is scaled by $\tanh$ into the box $\calW$, and it is trained to minimize the minimum critic at the action of the protagonist. During data collection, exploration noise perturbs the adversary output so that the replay buffer contains disturbance realizations around the current worst case.

\subsubsection{Curricula and Coverage}
Two curricula are employed to stabilize the adversarial training and to extend the horizon of the certificate. In the first curriculum, the adversary scale is ramped from zero to one, so the protagonist first learns to hold station in calm water and then faces a progressively stronger opponent. In the second curriculum, the discount $\gamma_h$ is ramped from 0.95 to 0.995, allowing the horizon of the certificate to grow as the value estimate improves. Every episode reset randomizes the full augmented state, including the gear level and the timer, ensuring that states entered in the middle of a lock are covered by data even though they cannot be chosen by the protagonist.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{One Step Learning of the Dwell Constrained Safety Value}
\label{alg_ris}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Initialize critics $Q_{h,1}, Q_{h,2}$, targets $\bar{Q}_{h,1}, \bar{Q}_{h,2}$, protagonist $\pi_h$, adversary $\mu$, replay buffer $\mathcal{B}$, adversary scale $\kappa \gets 0$, discount $\gamma_h \gets 0.95$
\FOR{each episode}
\STATE Sample $\xi_0$ uniformly over $\Xi$, including $g$ and $\tau_g$
\FOR{each step $k$}
\STATE $c_k \gets$ sample from $\pi_h(\cdot \mid \xi_k)$ with illegal gear levels masked
\STATE $d_k \gets \kappa\,\mu(\xi_k) +$ exploration noise, projected onto $\calW$
\STATE $\xi_{k+1} \gets F(\xi_k, c_k, d_k)$ with the plant ignoring illegal shift requests
\STATE Store $(\xi_k, c_k, d_k, \xi_{k+1})$ in $\mathcal{B}$
\STATE Sample a minibatch from $\mathcal{B}$ and form $y$ by \eqref{eq_target} with clipping
\STATE Update $Q_{h,1}, Q_{h,2}$ by regression onto $y$
\STATE Update $\pi_h$ by ascending \eqref{eq_actor} over the legal gear levels
\STATE Update $\mu$ by descending $\min_i Q_{h,i}(\xi, \pi_h(\xi), \mu(\xi))$
\STATE Soft update $\bar{Q}_{h,i} \gets (1-\rho)\bar{Q}_{h,i} + \rho Q_{h,i}$
\ENDFOR
\STATE Advance the curricula $\kappa \to 1$ and $\gamma_h \to 0.995$
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Certified and Deployed Sets}
After training, the certified set is defined as
\begin{equation}
\hat{\calS} = \bigl\{ \xi \mid q\bigl(\xi, \pi_h(\xi)\bigr) \ge 0 \bigr\},
\label{eq_certified}
\end{equation}
and the deployed set is the level set shifted by the margin, $\hat{\calS}_\tau = \{\xi \mid q(\xi, \pi_h(\xi)) \ge \tau\}$, where the margin $\tau > 0$ is calibrated by the adversarial Monte Carlo audit described in Section~\ref{sec_sim_audit}.

\begin{remark}\label{rem_scope}
Contraction, uniqueness, and invariance hold at the operator level. However, the value is represented by neural networks, and no bound on the approximation error is claimed here. Moreover, since $\gamma_h < 1$, the zero level set of the discounted fixed point is optimistic relative to the undiscounted value \eqref{eq_value}. For this reason, the deployed certificate is the shifted level set $\hat{\calS}_\tau$, whose margin $\tau$ effectively absorbs both the approximation error and the discount optimism, and every certified rate reported in this article refers to this audited level set.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Task Policy and Safety Shield}\label{sec_shield}

With the safety critics, the protagonist, and the adversary frozen, soft actor critic \cite{haarnoja2018} trains a task policy $\pi_t$ in a second stage. The reward combines the distance to the setpoint, a term that favors keeping a gear engaged over idling in neutral, a steering effort term, and a penalty on each intervention of the shield, and is given by
\begin{equation}
r_t = -w_1 \sqrt{\tilde{x}^2 + \tilde{y}^2} - w_2\, \mathbf{1}[g = \gN] - w_3\, \delta_c^2 - w_4\, \mathbf{1}[\text{shield active}],
\label{eq_reward}
\end{equation}
where $w_1$ to $w_4$ are the weights \pending{values}. The task actor has the same masked output structure as the protagonist, ensuring that it never requests an illegal shift.

Safety enters the second stage as a Lagrangian penalty on the certified value. The objective is formulated as
\begin{equation}
\max_{\pi_t}\ \mathbb{E}\bigl[ Q_t(\xi, \pi_t(\xi)) \bigr] + \lambda\, \mathbb{E}\bigl[ \min\{0,\ q(\xi, \pi_t(\xi)) - \tau\} \bigr],
\label{eq_lagrange}
\end{equation}
where $Q_t$ is the task critic, $q$ is the frozen minimum safety critic of Section~\ref{sec_learning}, and $\lambda$ is the multiplier adapted by dual ascent, respectively. To prevent the safety pressure from vanishing when the constraint is inactive, the multiplier is floored at a positive minimum $\lambda_{\min} > 0$, thereby keeping the task policy inside the deployed set during training.

At run time the two policies are combined by a value threshold filter. At each control step the task action $c^{t} = \pi_t(\xi)$ is evaluated by the frozen critic, and the applied action is given by
\begin{equation}
c = \begin{cases} c^{t}, & q(\xi, c^{t}) \ge \tau, \\ \pi_h(\xi), & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases}
\label{eq_shield}
\end{equation}
where the safety policy takes over only when the task action leaves the deployed set under the worst disturbance. The filter uses the same networks and the same margin as the certificate. \pending{Exact onboard switching rule to be confirmed against the deployed code, including whether the filter evaluates the task action alone or also the hold action.}

\subsubsection{Onboard Implementation}\label{sec_onboard}

The proposed controller runs as a ROS~2 node on the control PC of Fig.~\ref{fig_vessel}. The PID and NMPC baselines of Section~\ref{sec_baselines} run in the same node structure and differ only in the policy evaluation block, ensuring that all three controllers share the same state estimate, control period, actuation interfaces, and dwell enforced by the PLC.

As summarized in Table~\ref{tab_field}, the proposed controller achieved the best station keeping performance in terms of the mean and RMS distance and the time inside the disk. The PID controller B1 held station with a mean distance of 2.86~m and remained inside the disk for 78.5\% of the time. It left the disk only once, but this single excursion lasted 78.5~s and reached 5.66~m, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig_field_b1}(b), while the gear cycled regularly through forward, neutral, and reverse. The poorest performance was observed with the NMPC controller C1, which held station with a mean distance of 3.26~m and remained inside the disk for only 69.6\% of the time with a maximum distance of 7.52~m. In the run of Fig.~\ref{fig_field_c1}, the ASV left the disk ten times for 4.5 to 12.5~s at intervals of about 25~s, and in the shortest run it stayed outside for 59.0~s, which is consistent with the nominal disturbance handling of C1. Its computation time of 38.8~ms per step remained within the control period.

In contrast, the proposed controller held station with a mean distance of 1.91~m and an RMS distance of 2.17~m, and it remained inside the disk for 95.6\% of the time, with 3.0\%, 5.5\%, and 4.9\% of the time outside the disk in the three runs. Its excursions were short, and the longest lasted 12.0~s compared with 78.5~s for B1 and 59.0~s for C1, as illustrated by the four excursions of at most 6.5~s in Fig.~\ref{fig_field_prop}(b). The maximum distance of 6.06~m occurred in the last 10~s of one run, which ended before the ASV returned inside the disk, whereas every other excursion returned inside the disk within 12~s. Moreover, the three controllers shifted the gear at a similar rate of 10.03 to 11.03 times per minute, so the improvement was not obtained by shifting more often. In addition, the proposed controller engaged forward for only 2.8\% of the time compared with 20.7\% for B1 and 15.5\% for C1. These results confirm that the task policy and the shield trained in simulation transferred to the ASV without retraining and kept the ASV inside the disk for 95.6\% of the time under real sea conditions with a shift frequency comparable to the baselines.

\subsubsection{Discussion}\label{sec_exp_disc}

The behavior during locked intervals is the direct field evidence for the formulation of Section~\ref{sec_family}. The PLC enforced the dwell for every controller, and the measured minimum interval between shifts was from 3.48 to 3.50~s in every run, as listed in Table~\ref{tab_field}. During the locks the mean growth of the distance was the smallest for the proposed controller, 0.21~m compared with 0.26~m for B1 and 0.36~m for C1. However, its maximum growth of 1.73~m was the largest among the three controllers, indicating that a single lock opened at an unfavorable moment can still consume a large part of the margin. The gear usage also differed, as shown in panel (c) of each figure. The proposed controller held station mainly by alternating between reverse and neutral with brief forward engagements and spent 50.6\% of the time in neutral, whereas B1 and C1 cycled regularly through all three gear levels. Under the dwell, a reversal between forward and reverse must pass through neutral and therefore spans at least two locks. The baselines repeatedly committed to such full reversals, whereas the proposed controller mostly switched only between reverse and neutral.

The safety value $V_h$ in Fig.~\ref{fig_field_prop}(e) explains the interventions of the shield. Since $V_h \le h$ holds by construction, every sample outside the disk had $V_h < 0$. More importantly, in every one of the nine excursions over the three runs, $V_h$ had already fallen below zero before the ASV crossed the circle, on average 4.2~s earlier and from 0.5 to 7.0~s earlier. In addition, five of the fourteen episodes with $V_h < 0$ ended without any excursion. Therefore, the learned value anticipated the loss of the margin before it was realized, and $V_h$ was below zero during 4.4\%, 9.0\%, and 14.3\% of the three runs. The shield replaced the task action in 21.8\% of the logged status samples, which exceeds the fraction of time with $V_h < 0$ because the shield applies the margin $\tau = 0.5$ to the proposed action one step ahead, a stricter test than the zero threshold on $V_h$. Since the task policy is trained under the floored Lagrangian penalty of \eqref{eq_lagrange}, an intervention occurs only when the task action leaves the deployed set under the worst disturbance, so the shield rate measures how often the task policy approached the boundary of the deployed set in the field.

While the certificate is computed against the worst case adversary, the real disturbance encountered in the field is not a worst case. The wind speed during the trials was 5 to 6 knots, and the realized disturbance relative to the bound \eqref{eq_wbox} is \pending{realized disturbance level, if estimated from the drift during neutral locks}. The portion of the margin not consumed by the real disturbance acts as a reserve against unmodeled effects such as wave induced motion, estimation error, and actuator timing jitter. This reserve is desirable for a safety layer on an ASV that will meet stronger winds than those of the trial days.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec_conclusion}

This article presented a dwell constrained RIS framework for the safe weathervaning station keeping of an ASV driven by a single engine and a sterndrive. Since the dwell restricts only the control player of the safety game while the disturbance acts at every step, the safe set becomes a family of sets that shrinks with the remaining lock time. The gear state and its remaining lock time were augmented to the state, and the dwell constrained safety value was learned by a one step adversarial scheme with legality masking. A task policy and a value threshold shield were built on the learned value. The proposed framework was validated through simulations, \pending{simulation headline to be stated once the simulation numbers are final}, and through full scale field experiments with an 8~m ASV. In the field, the ASV remained inside the 4~m disk for 95.6\% of the time with a mean distance of 1.91~m, compared with 78.5\% and 2.86~m for the PID baseline and 69.6\% and 3.26~m for the NMPC baseline. The current approach provides no bound on the approximation error of the neural representation, and the field validation covers a single dwell channel. Future work will address these limitations by quantifying the approximation error and by extending the validation to several dwell channels.

\appendix[Proof of Lemma 1]

The proof of \cite[Theorems~2 and~3]{li2023cdc}, which builds on the discounted construction of \cite{fisac2019}, establishes the contraction and the monotonicity of the backup using only the following structural facts. The instantaneous term $(1-\gamma_h)h$ is fixed, the outer minimum with $h$ is nonexpansive, the minimization over $d \in \calW$ is nonexpansive, and the maximization over the admissible action set is nonexpansive, which requires only that the admissible set is nonempty and compact at every state. The admissible set is not required to be constant across states. Under \eqref{eq_legal} the set $C(\xi)$ is nonempty for every $\xi$, since holding the current gear is always legal, and it is compact as the product of a finite set and a compact interval. Therefore, writing $(\Phi V)(\xi) = \max_{c \in C(\xi)} \min_{d \in \calW} V(F(\xi,c,d))$, for bounded $V$ and $V'$,
\begin{equation}
\bigl| (\Phi V)(\xi) - (\Phi V')(\xi) \bigr| \le \|V - V'\|_\infty \quad \text{for all } \xi \in \Xi,
\end{equation}
and composing with the outer structure of \eqref{eq_backup} gives
\begin{equation}
\|\calT_h V - \calT_h V'\|_\infty \le \gamma_h \|V - V'\|_\infty .
\end{equation}
Monotonicity is inherited term by term, since each of the operations above preserves the pointwise order, and the Banach fixed point theorem on the complete space of bounded functions on $\Xi$ yields the unique bounded fixed point $V_h$. For robust invariance, let $V_h(\xi) \ge 0$ and let $c^\star \in C(\xi)$ attain the maximum in \eqref{eq_backup}, which exists by compactness. The fixed point equation gives $\min\{h(\xi), \min_{d} V_h(F(\xi, c^\star, d))\} \ge V_h(\xi) \ge 0$ after rearrangement, because $V_h(\xi) \le h(\xi)$ holds identically from the structure of \eqref{eq_backup}. Therefore, $h(\xi) \ge 0$ and $V_h(F(\xi, c^\star, d)) \ge 0$ for every $d \in \calW$, so the greedy legal action keeps the successor in $\calS$ for every disturbance, and the greedy policy belongs to $\Pi_C$ by construction. This establishes the robust invariance of $\calS$ under dwell constrained admissible policies. No step of the argument counts the switching channels, which gives Remark~\ref{rem_channels}. $\blacksquare$
